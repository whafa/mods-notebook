Public colorectal cohort funnel, October 2026

Issue: #590 · Track: #327 Script: backend/scripts/cohorts/cbioportal_funnel.py Data: the public cBioPortal API (portal v7.1.2, database 3.0.0), queried 2026-10-01. Every response was fetched between 11:18 and 12:04 PDT. MSK-CHORD’s cBioPortal import is dated 2026-07-30.

What this is for. Before building a “people like her” (Neighbors) view, count how many public patients have tumours that look like Maureen’s on somatic features, and check whether their treatment timelines are usable. A cohort like this is decision support and validation. It is never a repurposing signal: everyone counted here received standard drugs, and real-world exposure says nothing about drugs nobody gave them.

Summary

  • Her full somatic profile is not in public data. In MSK-CHORD the strict funnel runs: 5,543 colorectal cancer (CRC) patients, then 4,114 metastatic, 280 BRAF V600E, 181 MSS, 53 APC truncating and 2 ATM truncating. Neither of the last 2 is recorded as free of liver metastases, so 0 remain.

  • APC and ATM truncation are the wrong filters for a treatment question. They remove 99% of the cohort without changing which drugs apply. The usable cohort is BRAF V600E + MSS + metastatic: 181 patients in MSK-CHORD. Of these, 46 never had liver involvement recorded, 133 did, and 2 are unknown.

  • 98 of the 181 received encorafenib and/or an anti-EGFR antibody, and all 98 exposures have a start and a stop date. 39 also have a later regimen recorded.

  • Encorafenib specifically. Among BRAF V600E + MSS patients at any stage:

    • 71 have an encorafenib episode;
    • 58 of those had a concurrent anti-EGFR antibody, 26 of them with a MEK inhibitor as well;
    • median time on encorafenib was 155 days (IQR 80.5–290.5).

    66 of the 71 had systemic therapy recorded before encorafenib, so this is mostly later-line use.

  • Liver status at the start of encorafenib: 26 without, 45 with. These are the group sizes for the proposed pre-registered question below. I deliberately did not compute their outcomes.

  • MSK-CHORD is the only public source with usable treatment timelines for this question. Across all 34 public CRC studies there are 342 distinct BRAF V600E + MSS patients where MSS can be resolved, and 128 of them are outside MSK-CHORD. None of those 128 has a usable encorafenib or anti-EGFR duration. The other studies with timelines add 1 usable anti-EGFR course (TCGA, MSI unknown) and 1 encorafenib course with no stop date (HCMI, MSI unknown).

  • GENIE BPC CRC would add curated regimen lines and imaging-based progression. It needs a Synapse registration, which is Richard’s call. It probably adds few encorafenib exposures (see below).

All four MSK-IMPACT panels in the CRC set (341, 410, 468, 505 genes) cover BRAF, APC, ATM, KRAS and NRAS. Why “metastatic” includes the NLP site flags

MSK-CHORD has no single “metastatic” field. I tested three readings against an external yardstick: the 2,665 CRC patients who received a drug used in CRC only for metastatic disease. Those drugs are bevacizumab, irinotecan, regorafenib, trifluridine/tipiracil, cetuximab, panitumumab, encorafenib and ramucirumab.

The structured fields alone miss a fifth of demonstrably metastatic patients. By construction, the patients they miss are registry stage I–III with only the primary tumour sequenced. Their drugs say they had metastatic disease later. Of the 1,429 patients that no definition flags, 55 (3.8%) received one of these drugs, so the chosen rule rarely misses. Timeline rules

MSK-CHORD treatment rows are per-agent courses, with start and stop days counted from the sequencing date (day 0). The timeline has no absolute dates.

  • Episode. Rows of one class merge into an episode when the next row starts within 30 days of the current stop.
  • Index. The patient’s first encorafenib episode. A patient who never had encorafenib is indexed on their first anti-EGFR episode.
  • Time on therapy. Index stop minus index start. It is usable only if every merged row has a stop.
  • Regimen. Agents starting between 14 days before the index start and 14 days before the index stop. Supportive agents (leucovorin, bone agents, megestrol) are excluded.
  • What came next. The earliest regimen that starts after the index’s last 14 days and is either a new agent or a regimen agent restarted more than 30 days after the stop.
  • Possibly ongoing. The patient is alive, has no later regimen, and the index stopped within 30 days of last contact. This classification relies on OS_MONTHS sharing the timeline’s day 0. MSK documents that it does, and the data agree: none of the 214 timelines has an event later than 30 days after last contact.
  • Liver at index. A radiology report naming liver as a tumour site by index start + 30 days. “No” requires at least one NLP-read radiology report by then.

How each study encodes MSI:

  • MSI_TYPE: Stable / Instable / Indeterminate / Do not report. Used by the MSK studies.
  • MSI_STATUS: MSS / MSI-H / MSI-high / MSI / MSI-L / Not Evaluable / Inconclusive. MSI-L is left unresolved, not read as MSS.
  • MOLECULAR_SUBTYPE: MSS / MSI / POLE.
  • TCGA SUBTYPE and the MMR fields, as above.
  • Score-only fields: MSI_SCORE, MSI_SENSOR_SCORE, MSI_SCORE_MANTIS, MSI_PREDICTION_SCORE.

Overlap. The MSK studies re-use MSK-IMPACT patients. crc_msk_2026 is the largest genomic CRC cohort on the portal, and it holds 213 of MSK-CHORD’s 214 BRAF V600E + MSS patients plus 47 more, with no treatment data. The TCGA builds overlap each other. Counting each patient once, there are 342 distinct BRAF V600E + MSS patients across the studies where MSS resolves, and 128 outside MSK-CHORD.

AACR GENIE BPC colorectal: what it would add

GENIE BPC is not on the public portal. It needs a Synapse account and acceptance of GENIE’s terms of access, so whether to get it is Richard’s call. Here is what it would add, from #327 and the population planning doc.

  • Curated outcomes.
    • It holds 1,485 CRC patients in v2.0-public, from several cancer centres including MSK.
    • Curators abstracted it under the PRISSMM model: regimen-level drug data with dates, imaging- and oncologist-assessed progression, and so a real progression-free survival per regimen. That is the endpoint MSK-CHORD lacks.
    • MSI/MMR and PD-L1 are recorded.
  • Some patients outside MSK. MSK-CHORD is one centre’s referral population.
  • Probably few encorafenib exposures. To my recollection the CRC cohort was sequenced well before the April 2020 approval of encorafenib + cetuximab. That is unverified, so check the BPC data guide before counting on it. If true, BPC strengthens the anti-EGFR and chemotherapy questions more than the encorafenib one.
  • Overlap. MSK contributes to BPC, so some BPC patients are MSK-CHORD patients. Whether the identifiers allow a join is unknown until the data is in hand.

Proposed pre-registered question for a two-week Neighbors v0 spike

(2026-10-05: approved as decision D-Q4-13 on #614 and run once from the frozen code. Its results by liver group are not published.)

Question. Among MSK-CHORD patients with BRAF V600E, MSS colorectal cancer who started an encorafenib-based regimen:

  • How long did they stay on encorafenib?
  • What systemic therapy came next?

Answer both separately for those without and those with liver involvement on radiology at the start.

Why this one. Her disease is nodal without liver metastases, and she is on BRAF/EGFR maintenance. So the practical questions are how long such therapy holds and what usually follows it. Liver involvement is the subgroup her own record makes salient (mission spec §2.5).

Fixed in advance:

  • Cohort. BRAF V600E + MSS CRC patients in MSK-CHORD with at least one encorafenib row, under this script’s definitions. That is 71 at the 2026-10-01 query, and 69 of them also carry the metastatic flag.
  • Index. The start of the first encorafenib episode, with rows merged across gaps of 30 days or less.
  • Groups: liver at index, as defined above.
    • “With liver” means a radiology report naming liver by index + 30 days: 45 patients.
    • “Without liver” means none, though at least one radiology report was read by then: 26 patients.
    • The “ever” flag is not used. It would move 6 of the 26 into the liver group, because their liver disease appeared only after encorafenib started.
  • Primary endpoint. Time on encorafenib (index stop minus index start). An episode is censored when the patient was alive, had no later regimen, and stopped within 30 days of last contact (8 of the 71).
  • Secondary endpoints:
    • time to next regimen;
    • class of the next regimen;
    • the share with a positive NLP progression call after day 28 of the episode.
  • Analysis. A Kaplan–Meier median with a 95% CI per group, and the difference in medians with a bootstrap CI. It is descriptive: with 26 and 45 patients no test is powered, and none will be reported as one.
  • Reported by group, not adjusted for:
    • line of therapy (66 of 71 had systemic therapy before encorafenib);
    • regimen composition (with anti-EGFR, with a MEK inhibitor, with chemotherapy);
    • prior treatment outside MSK (19 of 71).
  • Freeze. The question, the definitions in this script and the analysis code are committed before anyone computes durations by liver group. This script stops at group sizes and pooled durations for that reason.

What the answer can and cannot say.

  • It describes how encorafenib-based therapy went for comparable patients in routine care at one centre, mostly in later lines.
  • It does not predict her course.
    • Her regimen began with chemotherapy for a recurrence and continued as maintenance, and she has no evidence of disease on it.
    • The cohort records disease status only through NLP calls, and has 24 encorafenib starts with concurrent chemotherapy and 2 that look like a first systemic therapy.
  • It is not causal. Liver involvement travels with tumour burden, line of therapy and performance status.

Limits

  • One centre, a referral population, and mostly later-line encorafenib. There are no absolute dates, so era can’t be adjusted for.
  • Rows record courses, not doses. 12 of the 100 index episodes are shorter than 14 days, which may mean single administrations or recording artefacts.
  • Encorafenib exposure is a floor. Masked investigational rows (27 of 214 patients) may hide trial BRAF or EGFR regimens.
  • Treatment before MSK is a flag, not a regimen list.
  • Site and progression flags are NLP model outputs. The liver flag misses at least 3.4% of sequenced liver metastases.
  • Time on treatment is a proxy for benefit, not progression-free survival.

Reproduce

These numbers measure a portal that reloads weekly, so a fresh run measures a different one. To replay this measurement, point the script at its snapshot in the main checkout:

python backend/scripts/cohorts/cbioportal_funnel.py --offline --cache-dir C:/Maple/backend/data/cohorts/cbioportal/snapshots/2026-10-02_590/http_cache
python backend/scripts/cohorts/cbioportal_funnel.py --json backend/data/cohorts/cbioportal/funnel.json
  • The snapshot is a 2026-10-02 re-run. The 2026-10-01 cache was lost when the worktree holding it was pruned. The 2026-10-02 live re-run reproduces this document: 344 of the 346 numbers in its results sections, and the other two are month conversions of day counts it carries. The snapshot holds its 702 cached responses and its output, funnel_2026-10-02.json. It is host-only and gitignored, because MSK-CHORD is CC BY-NC-ND. An offline replay on 2026-10-04 sent no request and matched that output exactly, apart from its own timestamp.
  • A fresh measurement (the second command) sends about 700 requests, one at a time with a pause between them, and caches them under backend/data/cohorts/cbioportal/http_cache/ in the checkout it runs in. Copy that cache into a new dated snapshot before the session ends. --refresh re-downloads.
  • Query window. Every printed study block carries its own fetch window in PDT.
  • The client is the shared one in backend/connectors/cbioportal_client.py (#667). Its cache key and entry layout are this script’s originals, so caches made before the switch replay unchanged. Since #667 the --json output is written with sorted keys, so an offline replay is byte-identical across runs.
