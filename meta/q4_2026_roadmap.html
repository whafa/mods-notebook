2. The thesis

Two spines run in parallel. The Research Steward drives both, and Richard’s time goes only to decisions.

  • Spine A: earn a signal. Make the base ranker beat popularity, then make personalization beat the base ranker. Judge both on labels with n ≫ 1 (cell lines, organoids), with RGCC as a secondary do-no-harm check.
  • Spine B: ship a list. An Options Ledger organized by decision horizon (Maintain / Prevent while NED / Prepare for recurrence). It is fed by trials, cohorts, measured combination data and the metabolic work, and by the model only once it has passed its gates. G1 never depends on the model.

Spine A alone repeats the last nine months: machine first, list later. Spine B alone abandons the project’s distinctive bet. B also gives A a destination, because every model candidate enters the Ledger with its evidence.

The lesson that shapes the gates: the project polished inputs (PRS) before testing the model that consumes them, and a ten-minute degree baseline would have exposed the model months earlier. So in Q4, every model number ships beside the strongest non-learned baseline for its surface, computed by committed code.

3. Spine A: earn a signal (Run #2)

3.1 Order of work

  1. S0, no retrain. Test #523’s shortcut hypothesis on the existing foundation.
  2. Evaluation rebuild (#593):
    • commit the baseline scorers;
    • write per-triple ranks and report per relation;
    • rank drugs given a disease (the G1 query);
    • partial ρ controlling for degree;
    • a validation curve;
    • zero-shot ULTRA as a baseline.
  3. Arm A. Retrain with entity_model.remove_one_hop: yes and nothing else changed (same export, same split fingerprint). About $115 and about 21 h.
  4. Arm B or B′, chosen by how A comes out:
    • if A passes S3/S4, Arm B is the relation-fidelity export (#592);
    • if A closes the shortcut but fails S3, Arm B′ is one diagnostic: more negatives (128 instead of 10), or an ULTRA-4g warm start. A warm start from ultra_3g cost −46% MRR on GP-KG before the evaluation fixes (agent_hallucination_log.md B3), so it is a diagnostic, not a default.
  5. Mechanism A in context (playbook §9.4). Score drug-grouped folds of her RGCC edges added to the fact graph at query time. There is no retrain, because ULTRA has no per-relation parameters. Judged against the drug-class-mean and degree baselines.
  6. Proxy benchmark (#591). Run PRISM first, then GDSC2, with a frozen leave-one-subject-out split and drug-mean as the baseline. Test simple conditioned models (kNN on expression, TabPFN) before #420.
  7. #420, the signed criterion. Only if a conditioned baseline beats drug-mean on the proxies. Weights start at 0, early stopping uses the criterion’s own loss, and λ is applied at score time.

Cap: two foundation retrains in Q4. Week-7 review (D19): if the best arm fails S4 and S6, foundation work stops. The KG then supplies evidence paths only, and the budget moves to proxies.

3.2 The baseline rule (D11)

Every model number is reported beside the strongest non-learned baseline for that surface, computed on exactly the same items by committed code. “Signal” means the paired-bootstrap 95% CI of (model − baseline) excludes 0. A result that is above zero but not above its baseline is written up as “no signal beyond popularity.”

SurfaceBaselines
Held-out link predictionper-relation tail frequency; degree; zero-shot ULTRA
Drugs ranked given a disease (G1 query)treats-subject count; degree
RGCC, per drawdegree (partial ρ given degree); drug-class mean
Proxies (across subjects)drug-mean (scores exactly 0 within-drug)

Per-relation reporting. Every held-out eval writes per-triple ranks. The stored table gives, per predicate (and per source_predicate once the export carries it), the n, filtered MRR and Hits@10 for the model and every baseline. The headline macro-average excludes interacts_with. The G1 query gets its own row.

4.5 Metro (#595): a priority lane

Decided 2026-10-05 (D-Q4-04, #612): Metro is not reduced.

  • Why. The Metro Map is the only Maple feature Maureen has asked about or researched. A useful Metro is the fastest way to get her using the system, and her use is the only user feedback the project will get. So Metro and the metabolic hypotheses are among the most important parts of the research. They sit beside PDR, not in place of it.
  • Scope. As much as current data and architecture allow. The therapy-adaptation layer, new metabolic sources and a patient metabolic model are back in scope where they fit. None of them is required this year.
  • Standing rule. Ask of any new work how it could feed Metro, and where Metro’s ideas could apply elsewhere: the Options Ledger, the G1 brief, model candidate lists.
  • The wider bet. The repurposing engine need not stop at single prescription drugs. It can be pointed at a combination of metabolic changes, brought on by diet, drugs or something else.

Order of work. The plan is on #595, as a starting point for Richard rather than a decision. In outline:

  1. Safe to read, and hers. Every station gets a badge against her current regimen, cited to the drug labels. Today the map lists itraconazole, a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor, and the Braftovi label says to avoid those. A plain-language key goes with it. “Ask about this” and “Add to my options” write to the Options Ledger; that is the feedback channel.
  2. Ground it. Every station→line claim carries a KG path through a target edge, or a citation checked against a primary source. An unsupported claim is shown as unsupported.
  3. Light it from her WTS percentiles against the Caris colorectal reference, for both colorectal specimens (the 2024 primary and the 2025 recurrence), with the caveats beside it.
  4. Widen it. Her systemic metabolism (labs first, then measurement-trait PRS after D-Q4-11), diet and supplement stations under the same safety screen, and combinations on the map with #594.
  5. Research. Stations suggested by the graph (judged against degree, D11), therapy-adaptation claims as cited claims, model scores once a foundation passes its gates, and a patient metabolic model as a stretch spike.

On “fundamentally metabolic.” D-Q4-04 doesn’t turn on this question. Metro is a priority because it is the surface she uses, and the testable form below is one of the things it should help test. Claude’s view, recorded for the record: the evidence doesn’t support it for her tumor.

  • Her tumor has a canonical driver, and the one intervention with a measured, dramatic effect targets that driver’s signaling loop. ctDNA went from 20.74 to 0.15 MTM/mL in about four weeks and has been negative since 2025-12-05.
  • BRAF V600E does rewire metabolism in targetable ways (Yun 2015, Science: high-dose vitamin C and GAPDH).
  • MAPK-blockade survivors are thought to shift toward oxidative metabolism. That is established in melanoma and open in CRC.

So the testable form is: her driver creates metabolic dependencies, and her maintenance therapy creates new ones. That lands on the NED question: what kills the cells her regimen doesn’t?

Appendix A. The questions behind this plan (Richard, 2026-10-01), condensed

  • More PRS. Count anchors, not scores. One slot per disease anchor: 109 today, about 180 reachable. NR policy alone gives +18; NR and palindromes together give up to +71. 2,133 measurement-trait scores have no slot in a disease-keyed loss, and placing them is a mapping decision. No lift is expected until #420 lands.
  • Biomarkers.
    • The 84 come from two Caris PDF panels.
    • The tumor transcriptome is WTS (RNA), not WGS, which is her germline DNA.
    • It is mechanically interchangeable with the UK Biobank inputs but semantically different: UKB used plasma proteins measured against healthy people; ours is tumor mRNA measured against other colorectal tumors. For ranking drugs against her tumor, ours is arguably the more relevant signal.
    • Axis v3 should be drug-target genes plus pathway scores, not all 17,604 genes.
  • The KG. Fix fidelity before breadth (#592, #438). Hetionet, DRKG and BioKG belong in pretraining (#121), not compositions. RTX-KG2 is parked unless an ablation earns it. The PDR2 paper’s released code also trains its foundation from scratch, so initialization isn’t the gap.
  • Combinations. Not representable today; in Q4 they come in as measured evidence with positive controls (#594).
  • Metro. See §4.5.
  • Similar patients and tabular research. See §4.3; neighbors only show what was tried, while PRISM-scale proxies can surface the untried.
  • Other treatments. The Options Ledger (§4.1).
  • The “move things forward” switch. The Research Steward (charter).
