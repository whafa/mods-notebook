Richard in the loop, 2026-10-21 to 11-04

This is the third audit, and the first under the six rules that took effect on 10-21 (#1147, charter §9a).

  • The first audit covered 10-01 to 10-16. It is §1 and §11 of the autonomy proposal, closed PR #996.
  • The second covered 10-16 to 10-21, in a review that isn’t published.
  • This one runs from the evening of 10-21 (PR #1148 merged) to the afternoon of 11-04, 13.97 days. It counts Maple only, and maple-studio has its own line.

In short

  • Rule 5 didn’t fire in these 14 days. All 13 new cards were marked Kind: his, and no technical card went ahead. One technical card reached the clock, TabPFN’s spec v5, on 11-04. Its wait ended that evening, after this doc closed, and its probe launched on 11-06, so the first firing falls just outside the window (§ After the doc closed). He called this rule “the most exciting and leveraging”, and said it “also carries the most potential for disaster”.
  • He sends Maple fewer messages: 20.9 a day, then 16.7, then 10.7. PRs merged fell far less, at 15.6, 13.5 and 12.5 a day. Vertex jobs went from 0.4 a day to 6.9, then 4.9.
  • Part of the fall isn’t autonomy. The steward was stalled for 59.4 hours, and 10-28, the one day he sent Maple nothing, sits inside the stall. He sent studio 17 messages that day and 24 the day before, so the stall and his move to studio can’t be separated. Outside the stall he sent Maple 12.4 a day.
  • His attention moved more than it shrank. Studio went from 1.5 messages a day to 18.8. Maple and studio together went from 22.5 a day to 29.3.
  • The steward’s list did not shrink. About 55 asks in 14 days against 60 in 16, or 3.9 a day against 3.8. The second window’s 7.5 a day has halved.
  • He decides new cards quickly: a median of 6.95 hours. Two cards took far longer: D-Q4-44 (Metro safety) at 192 hours and D-Q4-45 (TabPFN) at 106.
  • The refute review changes cards before he sees them. It dissented at least once on all 5 cards it read, and a lean moved on all 5. That shows activity, not that the reviews were right.
  • He still makes the largest calls. He changed 3 of 13 cards, and stopped the negatives pilot on his own machine after 134 hours, which no check had stopped.

  • “All his messages” is the fairer count. asks.py counts replies: it gives no row to a session’s first message, and doesn’t read one typed while a session is working (§ Method). The 21 typed mid-turn include D-Q4-40’s decision and “My machine is unusable”. Every Maple message row shows the same trend.
  • The interactive share includes his sessions’ subagents. Maple only, it nearly halved. The machine-wide figure hides that, because studio’s sessions all count as interactive.
  • “Merge” is matched as a whole word in any case. One of the 10 is a false positive (“there’s a red merge”), which leaves 9 real instructions. Three of those, all today, were his own files to merge: the roadmap twice and the charter once.

Rule 5: what went ahead

Nothing. Every digest that reports it says none went ahead, through 11-04. Three things explain that:

  • All 13 new cards are marked Kind: his, for reasons such as a fact about her, a gate changed after results, the fixer’s own code, a judgement mapping, a licence or a dissent. This audit reports those lines and doesn’t rule on each. D-Q4-54 most deserves a second look: it cites only “charter §2”, and §2 item 3 makes a gate defined before its data is seen a technical card. kNN’s S8 results had been seen, and §9a says a doubtful card is his, so it isn’t shown to be misfiled.
  • The first candidate was stopped by its review. The 11-02 digest: “#1311’s would have been the first, but its refute review dissented.”
  • The first to reach the clock is a spec, not a D-Q4 card: v5 on #1277, in a push reported sent on the morning of 11-04. The dry run that followed was reversible work, as §9a allows.

The lock had not arrived when this doc closed. On record at that point:

  • Its lock point is the probe’s launch, and nothing has launched.
  • Nobody has said “hold”, and he hasn’t decided the card.
  • The plan beneath it has moved. Fits took about 10 times longer than the spec’s inference, so the probe as registered can’t finish on his laptop. #1479 then took his machine out of use for compute. The L4 timing finished that day, and the T4 half waits until 11-05. What follows goes back to him with Part 2.

So the clock may end with no step for the lock to consume. It did, and the launch came on 11-06.

What this first firing shows:

  • The clock didn’t test the card. For most of the wait, the steward already knew the probe couldn’t finish as written. §9a doesn’t require a re-read while a card waits, so the wait passed anyway. The probe stayed down because the steward was waiting on the T4 timing, not because of anything in the rule, and D-Q4-59 followed.
  • He still chose where it ran. His wait covered the workstation version. What ran was on Vertex, on terms posted 32 minutes before launch. D-Q4-59 named the main changes and he approved those, but the addendum’s 13 items had no clock of their own. I don’t read that as a breach, because the change came from his own card.
  • The review didn’t miss this one. The Friday review notes that the dry run found the slow fits, not the review. The spec and its refute review both ran no model, so timing was outside what they checked. §9a sends a kind of card back to asking after a reversal by him or “an error a review should have caught”. I read this as neither, because D-Q4-59 took the steward’s own lean. That is my reading, and no digest has ruled on it.
  • “Hold” hasn’t been tested. Nobody has used it on a technical card. I searched every non-private transcript from the start of the clock to the launch, mid-turn messages included, and found no hold from him. The same search found “D-Q4-59 A”, so it can see his messages.
  • “Went ahead” has no stated meaning. Charter §9a lists a card that went ahead “with what consumed its lock point”, which reads as the lock being spent, yet the 11-05 digest used the words for a clock that had ended with its lock unspent. This section reads “fired” as the lock being spent. The charter is his, so the question goes to him in Saturday’s digest.
  • The dissent rail met its first case, and §9a doesn’t settle it. That morning’s digest put “spec v5 with its addendum” on the clock. The addendum’s refute review read DISSENT, posted on #1462 soon after, and within minutes the clock covered spec v5 alone, on its original times. §9a says a later review that dissents before the lock point holds the card. It doesn’t say whether a dissent on an addendum holds the spec beneath it, or restarts its clock. Richard decided that card (D-Q4-56) minutes later, so nothing turned on it here. That question goes to him too.

Failures

  1. The stall, 10-27 to 10-29 (59.4 hours). No tick fired after 10-26, and nothing reached him. Three nets failed in a row:

    • the session went dormant, and its cron fires only while the session runs;
    • the event watcher saw the missed ticks, but every relay failed on an expired Claude login, which also failed the nightly fixer on three nights;
    • the watchdog saw the stall from 10-27, but its push wasn’t sent, a limit recorded on 10-18 and never fixed.

    His own message ended it. Status comment, entry of 2026-10-29. The watchdog now emails him, and that passed its unattended test on 11-04.

  2. The negatives pilot on his laptop, 10-29 to 11-04 (134.4 hours). It ran on his own machine for five and a half days, and finished 10 of 18 runs. On 11-03 he wrote, “My machine is unusable.” His words on 11-04: “I really don’t ever want you doing this again.” The charter now says his machine, which it calls the workstation, is not a compute resource (#1479). No budget check could catch this, because it cost no money. pilot_860_v3 launched on Vertex that afternoon. After the window, it stopped on its own pre-registered rule.

  3. Metro, waiting on Richard. D-Q4-44 waited 192 hours. Its sentences were then put to him, and #1396 still waits on “sentences OK”. The frontend deploy follows it, carrying everything merged to frontend/** since 10-04.

  4. TabPFN, waiting on Richard. Part 1 took 106 hours. The checkpoint download was his own step, and came 110 hours after that, on 11-03. Both delays were his to give, and both pushed back a lane that was otherwise ready.

Method and limits

  • Sources: asks.py over every non-private transcript; 100 kept versions of the steward’s status comment; cards and their comments on GitHub; gh pr list; gcloud ai custom-jobs list; token_report.py, limited to Maple’s project folders for the Maple-only figures.

  • Repeats. Forked or resumed sessions repeat lines, so asks.py now keeps one row per reply time and text. Three re-sent messages escape that key, two in the first window and one to studio in the last. The reply rows keep them (254, 221), and the “all his messages” rows take them out.

  • Earlier first-window figures. A count of 246 replies came from a filter on session titles, which dropped 8 of his replies: 7 in the nightly fixer agent session and 1 in the dev changelog. They are real messages, so this doc keeps 254. The filter removes nothing from the later windows. A count of 46 “merge” replies has a separate cause, because those 8 replies don’t contain the word. Searching only each reply’s first 200 characters gives 46 / 3 / 10 exactly, and the full text gives 49 / 3 / 10.

  • Queued messages. A message typed while a session is working is stored as a queued_command attachment, and asks.py reads only user lines. I counted these separately and read the last window’s 21 by hand; all are his. Studio had 25 in the last window and none before. The tool does not read them yet (#1538).

  • Openers and back-to-back messages. asks.py gives a row only to a message that follows an assistant text, so a session’s first message, and one straight after another, get none. That missed 61, 16 and 15 for Maple, and 3, 1 and 19 for studio. The “all his messages” rows add them back, except:

    • 9 task briefs that read as written by a session, not by him (8 in the first window, 1 in the last). That is my reading of how they open;
    • 1 artifact block in studio;
    • 1 re-send on 10-04.

    Three messages went to both Maple and studio, and count once in the “together” row. Studio’s last window is 221 replies, 25 typed mid-turn and 19 with no row, less the artifact block and one re-send: 263.

  • Status versions. GitHub keeps 100, and they run from 10-24, apart from one on 10-01. That leaves 10-21 to 10-24 unrebuilt. The gap from 10-26 to 10-29 is the stall itself.

  • Elapsed time. The window crosses the clock change of 11-01, so every figure here is measured in real elapsed time, not wall-clock difference.

  • Private projects aren’t read: 4 transcripts, 28 with their subagents (GH #1327).

  • This audit is narrower than the second. It doesn’t sort asks into kinds A–E, and it doesn’t test rules 1–4 and 6 one by one.

  • The first audit’s 60 and this 55 are like for like: both count the steward’s list only. The second audit’s 57 counted other sessions too, so this table uses its steward count of 37.
