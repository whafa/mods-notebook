PDR2 Track A v2 — Variant-Aware Foundation Retrain and the Foundation-Quality Win

Authors: whafa, Claude (Maple project) Date: 2026-05-20 Status: Reproduction of PDR2’s Track A protocol against a freshly-trained foundation on a Maureen-focused composition (maureen_focus_v1). Foundation training, patient-conditional fine-tune, and three evaluations completed end-to-end on Vertex AI. Baseline (foundation-only) filtered MRR on Maureen’s outbound edges lifted +224% over the v1 (Colab-trained, primekg_civic_pharmgkb_v1) foundation. Personalization criterion at λ=0.01 still produced ~0% Δ (same architectural finding as v1); λ sweep run separately to find where the criterion becomes load-bearing.

  • docs/pdr_personalization_writeup.md — PDR2 personalization mechanism reference.
  • docs/PDR_PIPELINE_PLAN.md — master plan; this run closes the first end-to-end Vertex foundation→fine-tune→eval cycle on a variant-aware KG.

⚠ Correction, 2026-09-27 (GH #214, #433, #435). Two of this writeup’s headline readings do not hold.

  • The “+224%” own-edge lift is unrestated. Almost all of it sits on current_medication and sensitive_to_drug. This foundation’s training split held 74 of the patient’s 135 drug-sensitivity edges and 62 of her medication edges, so those are the predicates it had memorized. The patient MRRs were also computed on data the eval handlers re-exported and re-split, so “held-out” edges were largely training edges. The global filtered MRR of 0.565 is inflated as well: 60.6% of the test triples have their mirror in train.
  • The §4.4 “criterion weight signature” is initialization, not learning. Expression entered as raw 0–100 percentiles and saturated the loss, so the protein weights never moved from their random start. The v2 signature only looks on-target because the 84 genes it draws from are Caris’s actionable-oncology panel, and any subset of that panel reads like one an oncologist would circle.
  • The adjustment arithmetic in §4.5–4.6 assumes z-scored expression. The inputs were raw percentiles, so every adjustment was tens of times larger than the text states, and the λ at which the sweep turned destructive is correspondingly lower. The qualitative finding still stands: edge-blind boosting wrecks the ranking once it is strong enough to move it.

The foundation-training mechanics, the per-predicate λ-sweep behaviour and the architectural findings stand. A leak-free restatement is part of smoke run #1 (docs/mods_smoke_run_1_playbook.md).

Summary

On 2026-05-18/20 we built a new “Maureen-focused proof-out” composition (maureen_focus_v1: PrimeKG-tier2 + CIViC + PharmGKB + OncoKB + ClinVar with per-variant annotations), retrained the foundation model from scratch on it (Vertex A100-80GB, 21 hr, batch_size=64 settled by OOM probe), ran the Track A fine-tune protocol against the new foundation (synthetic_zeros PRS + real Caris expression, λ=0.01, 25 min), and evaluated baseline + personalized on Maureen’s 148 outbound canonical edges plus a full-ranking eval on the global valid+test splits.

The headline: the new foundation’s baseline filtered MRR on Maureen’s edges is 0.01671, up from the v1 foundation’s 0.00515 — a +224% lift. Hits@10 went from 0.0000 (v1) to 0.0203 (v2). Mean Rank dropped from 4524 to 3367. The lift is concentrated in clinically-actionable predicates: MAPLE:current_medication MRR went from 0.0038 to 0.171 (+4353%); MAPLE:sensitive_to_drug went from 0.0044 to 0.169 (+3764%); MAPLE:resistant_to_drug from 0.0028 to 0.014 (+382%). The two large negative movers (partially_sensitive_to_drug -56%, past_medication -14%) are the noisiest edge classes by construction — the v1 foundation was likely getting them right by chance, not by signal.

Personalization at λ=0.01 produced Δ_MRR = -0.01% (essentially zero), matching the v1 finding. The criterion weight magnitudes are ~0.02; with λ=0.01 and z-scored expression values, the per-entity adjustment is ~0.0002 — smaller than the raw model score variance. The λ sweep (0.10 / 1.00 / 10.0) is now complete and shows the criterion fires destructively at any λ strong enough to move the ranking (Δ_MRR -55% at λ=1.0, -82% at λ=10.0; all 11 predicates drop, with the predicates that the foundation ranks best dropping hardest). The mechanism is not learning which drugs Maureen needs — it’s blindly boosting the 1-hop-drug-neighbors of her high-magnitude criterion genes, which corrupts the foundation’s ranking. See §4.6 for the full sweep table.

Net: the foundation is what matters at this stage of the pipeline. The variant-level KG extension (OncoKB per-variant annotation + ClinVar cross-refs + the canonical drug-CURIE merge) translated into a ~3× MRR lift on the patient-specific task without changing the personalization mechanism. The PDR2 paper’s criterion mechanism not only fails to add lift on top — at any λ that’s strong enough to leave noise territory, it actively destroys ranking quality on a heterogeneous-predicate eval. Phase 12 (per-relation learned criteria) is the natural architectural fix.

The wallclock surprise. v2 took roughly 2× the wallclock of v1 despite the GPU upgrade (40→80GB) and the smaller graph (9.2M→4.55M edges). The root cause was a pipeline gotcha rather than a hardware regression: batch_per_epoch is a step count, not a sample count. When the bigger GPU let the OOM probe settle at batch=64 instead of v1’s batch=32, each epoch processed 2× more samples in the same number of steps — so total training samples doubled (320K→640K) for a fixed BPE. Per-sample throughput on the A100-80GB was actually ~equivalent to the Colab A100-40GB (~7.8 vs ~7.4 samples/sec); the extra wallclock bought 2× more training data, not slower training. Lesson saved as a feedback memory.

What the table is showing:

  1. The predicates with the highest baseline MRR collapse hardest at λ=1.0+. current_medication (MRR 0.17 → drops 78%) and sensitive_to_drug (MRR 0.17 → drops 71%) are where the foundation was performing best. The criterion swamps these by boosting all drug entities adjacent to high-criterion-weight genes (ATM, TP53, PIK3CA, CTLA4, etc.), most of which are NOT Maureen’s true current drugs.

  2. Non-drug-tail predicates (biolink:has_variant, biolink:has_phenotypic_feature) barely move even at λ=10. This is the drug-only mask working as designed — adjustment lands only on drug entities, so variant- and phenotype-tail queries see only the indirect cross-predicate-noise effect (boosted drugs pushing past true non-drug tails in the global ranking). At λ=10 this indirect effect is still small.

  3. There is no λ at which personalization improves the headline metric. The PDR2 paper’s mechanism (adjustment proportional to fixed criterion-weight × patient-value × strength=1) is not learning which drugs Maureen needs — it’s blindly boosting the connected drugs of her highly-weighted genes. For Maureen’s heterogeneous outbound predicate set, this is destructive at any λ that actually moves the ranking.

This is the empirical confirmation of the architectural ceiling the morning’s writeup (v1 §4.4, §7.3) identified — the criterion mechanism is well-suited to the paper’s drug-tail-only eval scope but fails on a clinically-realistic heterogeneous-predicate evaluation. The natural next experiment is Phase 12 (per-relation criterion architecture) — a separate learned criterion per predicate, which would let the model learn that ATM expression boosts PARP-inhibitor sensitivity but NOT past-medication probability. Out of scope for this run.

Per-gene-anchor diagnostic (deferred): to confirm the “boosts genes’ neighbors regardless of clinical relevance” hypothesis directly, one could enumerate the top-10 most-boosted drugs at λ=1.0 and check whether they’re 1-hop neighbors of the highest-magnitude proteins (ATM, PIK3CA, TP53). Strong prediction: yes, they will be. Worth a 30-min follow-up.

5. Architectural Findings — Carry Forward

5.1 The BPE / batch_size coupling gotcha

batch_per_epoch=500 is a fixed step count. Pairing it with a 2× bigger batch_size silently doubles the training-sample budget per epoch, which compounds across 20 epochs into 2× more total training samples. For matched-budget Vertex-vs-Colab comparisons, scale BPE_new = BPE_old × batch_old / batch_new. For “bigger is better” runs (this one), accept the longer wallclock and budget for it.

5.2 The pre-flight probe undersizes memory

training.py:1438-1473 runs the OOM probe under torch.no_grad(). Actual fine-tune training enables gradients, which on a 6-layer NBFNet stores ~6× more activation memory in the forward chain. A probe-passing batch can OOM in production. Cap FINE_TUNE_MAX_BATCH conservatively (we used 128 on the A100-80GB with the 4.55M-edge graph). The cleaner fix is to run the probe under torch.enable_grad() with at least one backward step — deferred.

5.3 OncoKB drug CURIE normalization required entity merge, not bridge edges

The OncoKB connector initially wrote synthetic oncokb:Vemurafenib-style entities when its node-normalizer couldn’t map to canonical CHEBI / DrugBank. Bridge edges (biolink:same_as) were not enough — PDR2 message-passing aggregates source evidence into a single node embedding, so bridges leave the duplicate alive and force the relation encoder to learn that same_as means identity. The merge approach (a567a52) repoints all relations and source rows from oncokb:X onto canonical CHEBI:Y and deletes the synthetic row. 53 entities (44 drug + 9 disease) merged this way, 39 of which materially affected our composition export.

5.4 Patient → variant resolver: name-match short-circuit didn’t attach maple_patient source

Variants already in CIViC by name (BRAF V600E) bypassed _upsert_variant_entity_for_maureen, which normally adds the maple_patient entity_source row. Without that row, connectors scoped by --scope maple_patient skipped the variant. Fix in c5c17a5 attaches the source row regardless of resolution path. Material impact: 16 new OncoKB edges on BRAF V600E (10 LEVEL_1 sensitivity) that wouldn’t have existed otherwise.

5.5 Disease resolution dropped from 6/13 → 2/13 (vs criterion)

The evaluate_personalized job’s disease resolver matched only 2 of 13 criterion-disease CUIs to KG entities, down from v1’s 6/13. With Track A’s PRS=0 this is empirically irrelevant, but Track B (with real PRS) would lose most of the disease channel. Root cause likely in how the composition export handles UMLS xrefs vs raw MONDO disease entities under our PrimeKG tier-2 prune. Audit deferred.

6. Open Questions and Risks

  1. Is the BRAF V600E lift the dominant signal? The +4353% on current_medication and +3764% on sensitive_to_drug are dramatic, but only 6 and 3 edges respectively. The CIViC chain on BRAF V600E (40+ drug edges) plus the 16 new OncoKB LEVEL_1 edges could plausibly account for most of the lift if a large fraction of Maureen’s actionable drugs route through BRAF V600E. A causal analysis (ablating BRAF V600E’s overlay edge and re-evaluating) would quantify this.
  2. The disease resolution regression (§5.5) needs an audit before any Track B run with real PRS.
  3. Phase 12 (per-relation criterion architecture) would address both the cross-predicate noise observed at λ=1.0 in v1 synthetic tests and the structural weakness of the criterion at default λ documented in §4.5. Out of scope for this run.
  4. RGCC Tier-1 validation against v2 (Spearman ρ between predicted score and RGCC sensitivity class on Maureen’s 224 drug sensitivities) needs a custom (h, r, t)-scoring script — eval_patient.json records per-predicate aggregates but not per-edge raw scores. Tracked separately.
