PDR Personalization Process — Technical Writeup

Authors: whafa, Claude (Maple project) Date: 2026-05-16 Status: Reference document. Describes the personalization mechanisms used by PDR1 (Oğuztüzün et al., J Biomed Inform 2025) and PDR2 (Oğuztüzün et al., J Biomed Inform 179, 2026) — verbatim from the upstream code — and audits Maureen’s data inventory against the inputs each model demands.

Summary

PDR1 and PDR2 personalize a knowledge-graph link-prediction model through two structurally different mechanisms. PDR1 puts the patient signal into the graph — every personalized fact (a PRS, a biomarker value, a diagnosis, a medication) becomes a per-patient self-loop edge, and the scalar magnitudes are loaded as a sidecar tensor that multiplicatively modulates the relation embedding inside forward_embedd. PDR2 keeps the patient signal outside the graph — the patient appears in the KG only as a node with a small set of WAS_DIAGNOSED edges, and the PRS / expression vectors enter only at fine-tune time through a personalized loss term whose learnable per-disease and per-protein weights ride on top of a frozen foundation model. Both models accept the same four discrete patient data types (PRS, biomarker expression, diagnosis history, medication), but PDR1 needs them shaped as a CSV row and a .npy scalar tensor, while PDR2 needs them shaped as a pickle dict and a per-patient overlay subgraph.

For Maureen we have abundant somatic data (84 nonzero gene-expression values from Caris MI Profile, 8 actionable somatic + germline variants across Caris / Invitae / Guardant, 224 RGCC drug-sensitivity readings) but the PRS column is presently all zeros because no genotype VCF has been uploaded — making Track A (synthetic_zeros PRS + real biomarkers) the only personalization track that runs today. The PRS pipeline (PGS Catalog connector + mods_polygenic_score table + 13-disease default scoring-file set) is built and waiting for genotypes; Track B unblocks the moment a 23andMe / WGS / VCF lands. Additional comorbidity / variant-level coverage is obtainable with targeted work — re-resolving Maureen’s HGVS-p strings through ClinGen, finishing the OncoKB / PharmGKB ingest, and ordering missing PGS Catalog scoring files — but the patient→variant edge fix and the OncoKB ingest are the two near-term unblocks for moving from “gene-level personalization” to the variant-level personalization that the somatic data actually merits.

1. Purpose and Scope

This writeup is a reference for what data each PDR generation actually consumes for personalization, in what shape, and where in the codebase that consumption happens. It is the document to consult before any future debate of the form “could we just feed Maureen’s X into PDR-Y?” because it pins down the exact tensor / table / column / line of upstream code that X has to land in for Y to use it.

Scope:

  • §2 catalogs the four discrete patient data types both papers use, with the source-of-truth column / field for each.
  • §3 documents PDR1’s scalar-modulated graph convolution personalization — the patient_merge.py self-loop construction, the scalars_<pid>.npy tensor, and the multiplicative use inside forward_embedd.
  • §4 documents PDR2’s frozen-foundation, learned-weights personalization — the pickle dict format, the PersonalizedRepurposingLoss, the patient-specific fine-tune (1,802 trainable weights), and the test-time apply_personalization adjustment.
  • §5 is a side-by-side comparison.
  • §6 audits Maureen’s data against both schemas — what is materialized today, what is held at zero as a controlled placeholder, what is obtainable with bounded follow-up work, and what is blocked on infrastructure / quota.
  • §7 lists open questions and risks.

Out of scope: PDR0 / KG-Predict, the in-graph link-prediction mechanics themselves (CompGCN+ConvE in PDR1; ULTRA+NBFNet in PDR2; both covered in pdr_phase0_gpkg_writeup.md and nbfnet_understanding.md), and our own Phase 12 / Phase 13 patient-as-node extensions (covered in paan_planning.md and temporal_patient_modeling_planning.md).

3. PDR1 — Scalar-Modulated Graph Convolution

PDR1 personalizes a single global model by rewriting the knowledge graph per patient. Every personalized fact becomes a self-loop edge (h, scalar, h) (where h is a disease CUI or gene symbol and scalar is the patient’s value for that feature), then a sidecar tensor of scalars is loaded into the model and used to multiplicatively modulate the relation embedding during message passing.

The unsqueeze broadcasts a [len(triples), 1] scalar column against the [len(triples), init_dim] relation embedding matrix, multiplying each relation’s embedding by the patient’s scalar value for that edge. Symbolic edges (scalar = 1.0) are unchanged; scalar edges scale by the patient’s PRS / expression magnitude. The Cohen’s d ≈ 1.05 lift the paper reports for PRS is the strongest of any single personalizer in their ablation. The mechanism is unambiguously the relation-embedding scale — there is no other personalization pathway in PDR1’s architecture.

4. PDR2 — Patient-as-Node + Frozen-Foundation Fine-Tune

PDR2 personalizes through a structurally different mechanism. The patient appears in the KG as a node (not just as a row in a CSV), but with minimal edges (WAS_DIAGNOSED only); the PRS and biomarker vectors are not put into the graph at all. They enter through the loss function during a per-patient fine-tune that freezes the entire foundation model and trains only 1,802 personalization weights.

4.1 Input shape — the patient pickle

utils.load_patient_data expects a dict with four required keys:

KeyShapeTypeSemantics
prs_values[len(disease_names)]np.float32Patient’s PRS for each disease, in the same order as disease_names
expr_values[len(protein_names)]np.float32Patient’s expression value for each gene, in the same order as protein_names
disease_names[len(disease_names)]list[str]UMLS CUIs — e.g. ['C0006142', 'C0011860', ...]
protein_names[len(protein_names)]list[str]HGNC gene symbols — e.g. ['TP53', 'BRCA1', ...]

PDR2 z-scores both vectors at load time (old_runs/run_drugrec.py:331–332), so the builder emits raw values.

The Maple builder is backend/scripts/build_pdr2_input_pickle.py — three source modes (synthetic_zeros, maureen, ukb_sample). The default 13-disease CUI set (CRC + 12 comorbidities) is sourced from backend/connectors/pgs_catalog_connector.py:261 (UKB_TRAIT_TO_PGS_DEFAULTS). Genes are sourced from mods_gene_expression with a test-type priority (Caris MI Profile > Caris WTS > RGCC qPCR).

4.2 In-graph representation — patient as a node, minimally

Unlike PDR1’s self-loops, PDR2 has the patient appear as a first-class graph entity with outbound edges. From the personalization mechanism reference: foundation training augments the GP-KG with patient nodes that each carry ~37 WAS_DIAGNOSED edges (in the original UK Biobank corpus). The foundation is trained on this augmented graph end-to-end — i.e. the model does see patient nodes during foundation training; it just sees nothing else about them beyond their diagnosis set.

PRS and biomarker values are not in the graph. They appear nowhere as nodes, edges, or scalar modulators. This is a deliberate architectural choice (and a major contrast with PDR1).

For Maureen, the canonical KG carries MAPLE_PATIENT:maureen-1173a8db with 250 outbound canonical relations materialized by backend/mods_patient_kg_service.py::materialize_patient_kg (the variant-aware KG extension) — already substantially richer than the 37-edge UK Biobank patient overlay, even before any of the variant resolver fix or OncoKB ingest lands.

4.3 The personalized loss — PersonalizedRepurposingLoss

losses.py:37 defines the personalization mechanism. The class holds two parameter tensors:

These are the only learnable parameters during patient-specific fine-tune. In the paper’s setup len(disease_names) = 341 and len(protein_names) = 1,461, giving 1,802 total trainable weights. The forward pass:

Total loss:

L_total = L_base(pos_scores, neg_scores)
       + λ₁ · tanh( ⟨w_disease_norm, PRS⟩ )
       + λ₂ · tanh( ⟨w_protein_norm, EXPR⟩ )

Both lambdas default to 0.01 in the constructor; the paper sweeps lambda1 (PRS weight) and lambda2 (expression weight) per-patient as documented at PDR_PIPELINE_PLAN.md L973.

What this loss is doing semantically. Each learnable weight tells the model how much that particular disease’s PRS (or that particular gene’s expression) should pull the next training-step gradient on the current triple. The tanh bounds the contribution to [-λ, +λ] regardless of how extreme the PRS or expression value is, preventing a single high-z-score feature from dominating. The L2 normalization on the weight vector keeps the magnitudes from drifting; only the direction of the weighting matters. After fine-tune convergence the learned weight pattern is effectively a per-patient summary: “for this individual, prioritize the bipolar-disorder PRS axis and the BDNF expression axis when ranking drugs.”

training.py:1193 — invoked from run.py when --use_ploss is set. Pre-conditions: a foundation checkpoint must already exist (trained on the augmented KG via train_foundation_model). Then:

All 168,705 foundation parameters become non-trainable. Only criterion.disease_weights and criterion.protein_weights (1,802 total) collect gradients. The fine-tune CLI from the personalization mechanism reference:

python script/run.py --patient_id X --dataset KG_ZORO \
  --use_ploss -l1 0.1 -l2 0.1 --ckpt <foundation>

Reported AUPRC lifts (paper §3): Robin 0.076 → 0.251 (3.3×), Zoro 0.277 → 0.368 (1.3×), Usopp 0.065 → 0.353 (5.4×). MRR / Hits stay similar because the relation-aware filter limits how much head-rank can move — the lift surfaces in AUPRC, which is unfiltered.

4.5 Test-time score adjustment — apply_personalization

A second personalization touch point exists at evaluation time: training.py:198 apply_personalization directly adjusts batch scores by a precomputed per-entity adjustment:

The mechanism iterates the graph’s edges, finds every drug node that is connected to one of the disease entities in criterion.disease_names (or to one of the gene entities in criterion.protein_names), and accumulates a score offset proportional to the learned weight × patient value. The offset is added to the model’s raw prediction at test time.

This is structurally similar to PDR1’s scalar modulation, except (a) it acts at the score level not the embedding level, (b) it uses learned weights from the fine-tune rather than raw scalars, and (c) it only counts drug→disease and drug→protein direct edges (no message-passing propagation). It is more like a post-hoc reweighting of the model output than a personalization of the model itself.

CRITICAL — λ is one-per-experiment, not separable across train and eval (corrected 2026-05-20)

The paper’s apply_personalization reads lambda1 = criterion.lambda1 directly from the criterion state — i.e., the same λ used during fine-tuning training rides on the criterion through evaluation (training.py:206-207). A “λ sweep” in the paper means N separate fine-tune → eval pipelines, each with one λ value baked in throughout. It does NOT mean “fine-tune once at λ=0.01, then evaluate the same checkpoint at λ ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 10.0}”.

Maple’s lib/pdr2/code/script/evaluate_personalized_maple.py accepts λ from the CLI (--lambda1_prs, --lambda2_biomarker), so it can be invoked with a λ that mismatches the fine-tune’s training λ. Doing this produces an off-spec eval-time-only sweep: the criterion weights are still those learned under the fine-tune’s λ, but the inference-time scaling is rescaled. This is not what the paper measures.

The May-2026 maureen_focus_v1 run did exactly this off-spec sweep — one fine-tune at λ=0.01, four evals at λ ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 10.0}. The reported Δ MRR ≈ −0.4% across all four evals is therefore suggestive, not conclusive. It tells us that no post-hoc scaling rescues the weights learned at λ=0.01; it does NOT tell us how a fine-tune at λ=10.0 (with weights learned under that stronger regularization) would perform. The eval-time-only sweep artifacts have been deleted from the DB (see scripts/strip_evaltime_lambda_sweep.py).

The paper-faithful path runs through Maple’s worker queue as N pairs of pdr_finetune_run + pdr_eval_personalized_run tasks, where the eval task defaults its λ to the source fine-tune’s training λ. Submit endpoints: POST /api/mods/research/runs/{foundation_id}/submit_finetune then POST /api/mods/research/runs/{ft_id}/submit_eval_personalized. Each fine-tune becomes its own mods_research_run row with parent_run_id set; the personalized eval attaches to the fine-tune (not the foundation), so the Overview matrix correctly fans out per-fine-tune in a sweep.

The structural takeaway. PDR1’s mechanism is richer per fact (every personalized scalar enters the convolution) but crude per patient (no learning, no per-patient parameters). PDR2’s mechanism is poorer per fact (only PRS and expression terms, only via a tanh-bounded loss adjustment) but richer per patient (it learns 1,802 patient-specific weights and freezes the foundation, which makes per-patient fine-tune cheap enough to do at clinic scale). Phases 12 (patient-as-node V2) and 13 (temporal modeling) are designed to combine the per-fact richness of PDR1 with the per-patient learning of PDR2 — see paan_planning.md.

7. Open Questions and Risks

Q1. Does PDR1’s mechanism transfer to oncology? UK Biobank PRS was developed against late-onset chronic disease (AD, CAD, T2D) where polygenic accumulation explains much of risk. For mCRC, somatic-driver biology (KRAS / BRAF / MSI) probably dominates the actionable signal far more than germline polygenic risk does. If Maureen’s Track B run shows no Tier 1 ρ lift over Track A, that’s not necessarily a wiring failure — it may be that PDR1’s scalar mechanism is a poor fit for an oncology patient. (PDR_PIPELINE_PLAN.md L924–925 documents this as the explicit Phase 9 fallback decision.)

Q2. PDR2’s apply_personalization only counts direct drug↔disease and drug↔gene edges. Multi-hop reasoning (drug → target → pathway → disease) is the strength of NBFNet message passing in PDR2’s base model, but the test-time score adjustment doesn’t propagate through it — it only sees adjacency. This may understate the lift from learned weights. We have not audited what fraction of Maureen’s clinically relevant drugs are directly connected to her diagnoses / expressed genes in PrimeKG vs reachable through intermediate entities.

Q3. The 1,802-weight ceiling. PDR2’s personalization has at most len(disease_names) + len(protein_names) learnable weights per patient. For Maureen with the 13-disease default and ~84 genes, that’s 97 weights, not 1,802. The cohort the paper trained against had richer disease / gene vocabularies via the UK Biobank Olink panel. Whether 97 weights is enough to capture Maureen-specific signal in a 3-epoch fine-tune is empirically unknown.

Q4. Patient-network leakage. PDR2’s foundation training augments the KG with all patients during foundation training, then fine-tunes per-patient. If we eventually move toward a population tapestry (Scenario Z, paan_planning.md) the foundation could see patient↔patient similarity via shared neighbors, opening test-set leakage if validation isn’t held out by patient ID. PDR2’s paper handled this implicitly by training on a fixed UK Biobank cohort; we’d need an explicit hold-out policy.

Q5. Variant vs gene granularity for biomarker expression. PDR1 and PDR2 both key expression by gene, but Maureen’s actionable variants (e.g. BRAF V600E vs wild-type BRAF) have radically different drug-response biology. A gene-level expression of BRAF tells you nothing about V600E status. The full architectural fix is variants-as-nodes (Phase 8.5) — and that fix is needed for the variant-level edges first, not for re-keying expression. Re-keying expression by variant is an open research question for Phase 12+.

Q6. Phase 1 quota grant. PDR2 training automation is code-complete through aiplatform.CustomJob.submit() but blocked on Vertex AI L4 / A100 quota grants (an internal note on the training-automation status). Without the grant, the per-patient fine-tune machinery cannot be tested end-to-end against Maureen’s pickle. Smoke test command is staged.
