MODS — Mission Spec

A goal-oriented briefing for an agent arriving with no prior context.

Version: 1.0 · Written: 2026-09-07 · Repo: whafa/Maple

0. What this document is, and how to read it

Every other planning document in this repository is organized around a system: the knowledge graph, the training pipeline, the PRS gates, the dashboards. This one is organized around the goal. It exists because the systems are now complex enough that a capable agent can spend a week doing excellent work on a subsystem that does not move the actual objective.

Read it in order. §1–§3 are the why and the who; skipping them will cause you to make locally-reasonable decisions that are globally wrong. §4–§6 are what has been built and what it has actually shown, stated honestly including the null results. §7 is the rules that were paid for in lost data or wrong clinical claims. §8 is the open frontier, ordered by expected value to the patient. §9–§11 are orientation.

This document goes stale. Anything with a number in it should be re-verified against the live source named beside it before you rely on it. §10 tells you how.

The one-line summary, if you read nothing else: we are trying to find drugs that might work for one specific woman with stage IV colorectal cancer, using a biomedical knowledge graph and a graph neural network conditioned on her own molecular data, and to do it rigorously enough that an oncologist would take the output seriously.

1. The mission

1.1 The objective

Extend Maureen’s life, and the quality of it, by finding treatments for her disease that the standard of care will not surface on its own.

That is the top of the goal tree. Everything below it is instrumental. The project is not “reproduce a paper,” not “build a knowledge graph,” and not “ship a dashboard,” although it has done all three. When a decision is genuinely ambiguous, resolve it by asking which branch gets closer to a defensible, actionable candidate list for this patient.

Any plan that does not shorten the distance to one should justify itself.

1.3 The precedent this is modeled on

David Fajgenbaum, as a medical student, was dying of Castleman disease with no approved treatment and nobody working on a cure. He worked out that sirolimus, a 25-year-old immunosuppressant approved for something else entirely, should work on his pathway. He tried it. He is alive. He co-founded Every Cure in 2022 to do that systematically, scanning roughly 22,000 diseases against 3,000 drugs.

Richard heard Fajgenbaum tell this story on the Huberman podcast on 2025-11-03. That is the origin of this project — in his words, “it flipped some switch in my head.” When framing MODS in a writeup or an intro, anchor on Fajgenbaum and sirolimus, not on generic “AI for drug discovery.”

The bet MODS makes is the n-of-one version: among the ~20,000 approved drugs there may be one that nobody has thought to try for colorectal cancer, or for this colorectal cancer, and a knowledge graph plus a link-prediction model is a defensible way to look for it.

1.4 What this is not

  • Not medical advice, and not a substitute for oncology. Maureen is on the standard of care and will stay on it while it works. Nothing produced here reaches her without going through her treating physicians.
  • Not a decision maker. A high model score means “the literature’s geometry is consistent with this drug being relevant.” It does not mean the drug works, is safe in combination with her current regimen, or is compatible with her liver function and blood counts.
  • Not a product. There is one user and one patient.

The value, if there is value, is precision. A tool that surfaces one surprising, plausible candidate her oncologist has not considered is worth building. A tool that surfaces fifty is not.

2.5 The single most important clinical fact for repurposing

Her recurrence is nodal-predominant with no liver metastases.

Active liver metastases are the strongest negative predictor for immunotherapy in MSS colorectal cancer; the mechanism is that liver involvement induces systemic immune tolerance. The no-liver-met subgroup is not a post-hoc slice, it is the entire efficacy story for checkpoint blockade in this disease.

Botensilimab + balstilimab (Fc-enhanced anti-CTLA-4 plus anti-PD-1) in refractory MSS mCRC without active liver metastases: ORR 20%, median duration of response 16.6 months, median OS ~21 months, 2-year OS 42%, 3-year OS 33% (n=123, phase 1b/2 C-800-01). For context, the expected ORR from checkpoint inhibitors in refractory MSS CRC is approximately zero.

Use this as the interpretive key when reading any MSS-CRC immunotherapy result: always ask what fraction of the cohort had liver metastases.

Caveats, current as of 2026-08-29 and worth re-checking:

  • Not FDA approved; the agency discouraged the accelerated-approval pathway.
  • The phase 3 BATTMAN trial (NCT07152821) is TERMINATED as of 2026-07-13, whyStopped = “Pharmaceutical Sponsor discontinued trial financial support.” Agenus press releases announcing enrollment and a $340M raise are still live and still wrong. Sponsor financial durability is a material risk to the whole program.
  • France granted reimbursed compassionate access (AAC, ANSM, 2025-09-09) for exactly her phenotype. France-only; applicability to a US patient is unestablished.
  • NCT07227636 (MSK, adjuvant BOT/BAL for ctDNA-positive CRC) is the closest fit to her situation but its cohorts require R0 resection of stage III disease or of a liver metastasis; stage IV nodal-only may map to neither.

The NED eligibility gap. This will keep recurring. The highest-value unblocking action is tumor-informed ctDNA testing (Signatera) — CT resolution bottoms out around 5 mm, ctDNA positivity is the entry criterion for every MRD trial, and it informs maintenance duration regardless. 3. The approach, and why it is shaped this way

3.1 The core idea in plain terms

A biomedical knowledge graph is a map of facts: drug X treats disease Y, gene Z is associated with disease Y, drug X targets protein P. Do this for a few million facts and you get a web connecting drugs, diseases, genes, proteins, phenotypes and pathways.

A graph neural network can learn the shape of that map — not the facts, the geometry. Once it has the geometry you can ask about edges that are not written down: if this drug and this disease are not linked in the data, how strongly does the geometry say the link should exist? That is the repurposing signal.

Then you condition it. A model trained on published facts gives the best answer the literature supports for “colorectal cancer” as a category. That is not what is needed. It has to be the best answer for this tumor, with its specific mutations, its specific expression profile, and its specific history of what has already been tried. That conditioning is the hard part, and it is where the project currently is.

3.3 Why two knowledge graphs

  • GP-KG (61,097 entities, 1.24M relations) is the paper comparator only. If the pipeline reproduces the published numbers on the data the papers used, the tooling is trustworthy. It is insufficient for the real work: only 1 of Maureen’s 5 high-sensitivity RGCC drugs is in it, and most biologics are missing entirely.
  • PrimeKG (129,157 entities, 7.99M relations) is the production graph. It contains the drugs she is actually on, and it carries indication, contraindication and off-label-use edges that most graphs lack — exactly the edges repurposing needs.

Agreement between the two on a candidate is a stronger signal than either alone.

3.4 Why a graph neural network rather than a language model

Large language models are trained on human language, not molecular language, and are weak at the chemistry and biology this requires. The knowledge graph gives explicit, traceable, multi-hop structure; the GNN learns over that structure; every prediction can be walked back to the edges that produced it. Interpretability is not a nicety here — a candidate that cannot be explained to an oncologist is not actionable.

5. Where the science actually stands

This section is the honest ledger. Read the null results as carefully as the positive ones; they are the reason the project is where it is.

5.1 Paper reproduction (Phase 0) — mostly closed, one asterisk

GP-KG loaded and trained; ranking correlation against the published drug ranking is Spearman ρ = 0.740. Strong, not the ≥0.90 that would let us claim full reproduction. The gap is not a bug: the authors never shipped their train/test split and their split script sets no random seed, so the exact split is unrecoverable.

The Alzheimer’s case study sits at AUROC 0.79–0.81 against the paper’s 0.868, a ~7% gap from the same cause. Open decision: chase it or document it as an asterisk.

5.2 Architecture — a real finding

Vanilla CompGCN cannot go deeper than about 2 layers on PrimeKG. At average degree 125, a 2-hop neighborhood already covers 12% of the graph and a 3-hop one saturates it; this is textbook over-smoothing, and 1 layer empirically beats 2 by ~12%.

Depth is an architectural problem, not a hyperparameter one. Within CompGCN, embedding dimension is the lever, not depth. NBFNet and ULTRA use a labeling trick that makes per-query rooted features rather than whole-graph smoothing, which is why they scale to 6 layers on dense biomedical graphs. This is the justification for the whole PDR2 move.

5.4 Personalization — the central open problem

The published PDR2 personalization mechanism does not, as released, produce lift on Maureen’s data. This is the most important negative result in the project.

PDR2 personalizes through two independent mechanisms that only combine at inference:

  • Mechanism A, patient-as-a-node. Maureen is a first-class entity in the training graph from epoch 1, so the foundation learns an embedding for her and queries of the form (Maureen, r, ?) are possible. This works and is required for anything else.
  • Mechanism B, the personalized loss. The foundation is frozen; the only trainable parameters are per-disease and per-protein weights inside the loss, multiplied by her polygenic risk scores and expression values. At test time these produce an additive bias on each candidate’s score.

Measured results on her Track A profile:

Four things are known about why:

  1. The released loss and the paper text disagree in four places: sign on the λ terms, per-triple coupling to the prediction score, a tanh, and L2 weight normalization. Implementing the paper text literally is catastrophic. The tanh and normalization are load-bearing stability tricks the text omits.
  2. The criterion is gradient-degenerate. Both loss terms are functions purely of (criterion weights, fixed patient values) and do not depend on the batch, so their gradient is identical at every step. The optimum is reachable in closed form; a 20-epoch fine-tune buys nothing after the first few.
  3. The criterion is edge-blind. In apply_personalization, adjacency strength is hardcoded to 1.0, so a sensitive_to edge and a contraindication edge push the same direction. The identified fix is a learned signed per-relation term (Phase 12, GH #47, design in docs/pdr2_phase12_per_relation_criterion_planning.md). It requires no foundation retrain.
  4. The genetic half has never been fed real numbers. Every run to date used “Track A” — zero PRS. Disease weights stayed at random initialization because with prs_values = 0 there is no gradient. In the PDR1 paper, PRS was the single highest-impact feature (Cohen’s d = 1.05, dropping to 0.77 without it). We have been running the personalization with its most important input set to zero. (2026-09-28: the first release of her PRS: a v0 smoke release of 590 scores over 109 disease anchors (tag smoke_v0, release run c3be9df0), normalized against the 667-sample EUR reference. Smoke run #1’s Track B fine-tunes are the first to feed it; §5.5.)

5.5 Polygenic risk scores — rebuilt, not yet computed

The PRS work is a cautionary tale worth understanding before you touch it.

The original scorer read a variant-only genotype store and treated an absent locus as dosage zero. For a scoring file whose effect allele is the reference allele — about half of all loci — a homozygous-reference call should contribute 2 × weight, not 0. Every score in the legacy fleet was wrong. The fleet was wiped on 2026-07-13. An external audit independently reproduced the ~49% drop across 40 scores.

One decision sits between E2 and release. Gate C defers palindromic SNVs (2.99% of rows, but a median 10.4% of rows for each of the 1,687 affected scores), and their strand can only be resolved from panel allele frequencies. Resolved (Richard, 2026-09-22): run both, every time, and mine the divergence. This document used to say pgsc_calc v2.3.0 was “the scoring engine of record, not the bespoke scorer”, while docs/prs_primer.md defined Gate C as building the bespoke scorer and reconciling it against pgsc_calc. That either/or was the wrong frame. Both run on every scoring pass; the delta between them is a first-class output, not a one-time acceptance test.

The reason this is stronger than picking one: the July audit’s lesson was that a check sharing the scorer’s assumptions cannot fail. A permanent second opinion is the structural answer to that, where a one-off gate is only a snapshot. And the divergence decomposes — pgsc_calc mean-imputes missing dosages to 2p and resolves palindromes by allele frequency, both of which MODS deliberately does not do (§ prs_primer.md 5.4). So each per-score delta splits into an imputation term, a palindrome term, and a residual that should be zero. A non-zero residual points at a real defect in one of the two calculators; the imputation term separately quantifies how much of a published score’s value is invented rather than measured, which is worth knowing about a score no matter who computed it.

Division of labour that follows: MODS is canonical for the released value (it is the only one that emits the coverage_v1 payload the read layer renders, and it is reproducible in-repo), pgsc_calc is the independent witness, and agreement within tolerance is a Gate F release criterion. backend/scripts/prs/reconcile_pgsc_calc.py already exports the bundle and diffs the sums; what it still needs is Nextflow and a container runtime — plausibly the existing digest-pinned Vertex image_uri seam. Smaller question still open: where the per-score delta is stored (a reconciliation_v1 block in the score’s metadata is the obvious home, and needs no migration).

The other trap here, closed in code on 2026-09-28 (GH #244): the 13-entry list then named UKB_TRAIT_TO_PGS_DEFAULTS silently sized the model’s disease axis (schema v1: 6 diseases, no colorectal cancer), and three of those 13 are labeled for the wrong disease. It is now SMOKE_TEST_PGS_SET, used only where it is asked for by name. Axis v2 is built from the released Gate F axis picks, one position per anchor, with colorectal cancer’s MONDO:0005575 required, and v1 is never rewritten. Gate C does not use the list either — an omitted pgs_ids resolves to the Gate B manifest’s production-eligible set.

Ancestry note: Maureen is almost entirely European, corroborated from her own WGS at ancestry-informative loci. European-trained scores are best-case portability for her, so no elaborate per-score portability flagging is needed.

5.6 A validation flaw you must know about

mods_patient_kg_service._build_drug_sensitivity_edges materializes her RGCC sensitivities as MAPLE:sensitive_to_drug edges on her patient node. Any foundation exported with --include-source maple_patient therefore trains on the RGCC data we then validate against. The reported Tier-1 and Tier-2 numbers are not a clean holdout. Tracked as GH #214.

Quantified 2026-09-27. The de-facto foundation’s training split held 74 of 135 RGCC edges and 62 medication edges; §5.3 has what that did to the v2 numbers. The export also splits PrimeKG’s mirror pairs apart: 60.6% of that foundation’s test triples have their reverse in train, which inflates every PrimeKG foundation MRR (GH #432).

The design of record is: train on her orthogonal biology — genetics, expression, PRS, variants — hold RGCC out entirely, and trust the ranking only if it recovers her measured sensitivities. The fix is roughly one export flag. (2026-09-27: it is not one flag. The overlay branch applies no predicate filter, and every downstream handler re-exports and re-adds the overlay (GH #432, #433). The patient query also has to change: a foundation trained without these edges cannot score MAPLE:sensitive_to_drug at all.)

6. Validation doctrine

There is no ground truth for “which drug will work for Maureen.” There are proxies, and their quality determines what can be claimed.

6.1 The three tiers

RGCC gives 224 quantitative drug-sensitivity measurements on her own circulating tumor cells — 112 distinct drugs across two draws. This is the colorectal analogue of the papers’ Alzheimer’s case study, but stronger, because it is patient-specific and continuous rather than population-level and binary.

Three properties of this label set govern what can be claimed from it, all established 2026-09-24 (GH #317):

  • Two grading schemes, one column. 59 of the 112 per report are graded on a percentage scale; the other 53 (MoAb, SMW) come off a binary “No sensitivity / Sensitivity” chart encoded as 0. They are real negatives, but a Spearman over all 112 carries 53 pairs tied at zero. Report Tier-1 both ways — all-panel and graded-only.
  • The two draws are not replicates. All-panel test-retest is ρ = +0.9616 (n = 112); graded-only is ρ = +0.7635 (n = 59, mean |Δ| 14.5 points). The panel is globally stable, but 12 drugs move ≥25 points and they move as two coherent classes — four taxanes down (Abraxane 75→15, Paclitaxel 75→22, Docetaxel 70→20, Cabazitaxel 70→14) and eight anthracycline / topoisomerase-II agents up (Doxorubicin 24→79, Etoposide 22→71, Epirubicin 20→67, …). RGCC says so itself: its page-1 summary lists “Partial sensitivity: taxanes” in February and “inhibitors of topoisomerase I, inhibitors of topoisomerase II” in September, with taxanes dropped. That straddles the Aug 2025 recurrence and the BREAKWATER start.
  • Therefore: never pool the draws. Averaging sends both classes to mid-range and erases the only real longitudinal signal in the set while leaving the other 100 drugs almost unchanged. compute_metrics_crc.py now evaluates per timepoint and prints the test-retest figures on every run.
  1. Tier 1 — Spearman ρ between model score and % cell death. Works on any checkpoint, no fine-tuning. Most permissive test.
  2. Tier 2 — AUROC/AUPR with >80% cell death as positives, <35% as negatives. Direct analogue of KG-Predict’s AD case study.
  3. Tier 3 — leave-one-out personalization. Hold out one responsive drug, fine-tune on the rest, check whether the held-out drug climbs. Only feasible on PDR2, where fine-tuning is cheap.

Tiers 1 and 2 validate the base ranker. Tier 3 validates whether personalization adds anything, and is only worth running once the base ranker shows nonzero correlation.

6.2 What must be disclosed every time RGCC is cited

  • In vitro circulating tumor cells at fixed drug concentrations, not in vivo response.
  • Prodrugs such as capecitabine will underperform relative to their active metabolite.
  • Two time points, which must be reported separately rather than averaged.
  • Non-uniform panel coverage.
  • Correlation is supportive, never conclusive.
  • Single agents only, so it cannot see her working regimen. Encorafenib and binimetinib are not on the panel. The BRAF/MEK inhibitors it does test (dabrafenib, vemurafenib, trametinib) score 0 on both draws, and cetuximab scores 10 → 15. Yet her ctDNA fell from 20.74 to 0.15 MTM/mL between the 2025-10-01 and 2025-10-27 draws, after the combination began. In colorectal cancer, BRAF inhibition works only when EGFR feedback is also blocked (Prahallad 2012). So a ranker that correctly puts BRAF/MEK/EGFR agents high is penalized by RGCC; flag or exclude them in Tier-1/Tier-2. (Added 2026-09-28.)
  • RGCC’s own outcome study. Guadagni et al., BMC Cancer 2022, co-authored by RGCC: 36 refractory CRC patients treated with RGCC-selected drugs. PPV 47%, specificity 5%, overall accuracy 47%. The design is selection-biased, but it is the only outcome validation the assay has. (Added 2026-09-28.)

6.3 Other validation surfaces

  • Cross-KG agreement. A candidate scoring high on both GP-KG and PrimeKG is more trustworthy than one scoring high on either.
  • Her medication history. The models should rank drugs she was actually prescribed and responded to above random. 14 response episodes are labeled. (⚠ 2026-09-27: circular on every foundation trained so far. All 14 episodes are “beneficial”, every drug involved is a tried_drug target, and 7 of the 8 had a patient→drug edge in the training split. The surface is usable only on a foundation exported without the medication predicates, and even then, with no negative episodes, it tests rank-above-random only.)
  • Literature review of top candidates. What the PDR2 paper did. Weakest form, but it is the last gate before anything goes to a clinician.

CV MRR is a poor proxy for any of this. Training-time metrics do not predict patient-specific generalization. Validate against RGCC directly.

One more warning: never trust an upstream evaluation function without auditing what it computes. PDR2’s own validate() reported MRR ≈ 0.99 because of a relation-aware filter bug. Use eval_maple.py full-ranking evaluation for any cross-method comparison.

7. Rules

These were paid for. Several cost real data; two produced wrong clinical claims.

7.1 Clinical truth

  • Trace every patient fact to her data. A driver that “sounds typical” for colorectal cancer (KRAS, TP53) is exactly the failure mode. Cross-check mods_list_alterations and follow alteration.document_id to the source report.
  • Trial status comes from the ClinicalTrials.gov v2 API, never from press releases, sponsor sites, or news coverage. Read overallStatus and whyStopped. A wrong trial status can send Maureen and her oncologist chasing a dead program.
  • VAF across specimens is not a trajectory. Variant allele fraction scales with tumor purity and copy number. Every variant rising together across her two Caris reports is the signature of a purity shift, not three clonal expansions. Use serial plasma ctDNA for trajectory.

7.2 Data integrity (hard rules, from CLAUDE.md)

  • Never collapse rows that look like duplicates but carry distinct semantics. A (subject, object, source) triple appearing with two source_predicate values is the source asserting two different things. A prior migration silently deleted 15,045 rows this way and lost the drug-metabolism distinction from the canonical graph.
  • Never decide an ontology or vocabulary mapping that is not crystal clear. Propose the mapping as a table with the judgment calls flagged, get approval, then commit. biolink:related_to is the giving-up predicate.
  • Any new edge must preserve source_predicate.
  • Register every schema change in backend/migrations.py. Never ship a standalone .sql file. Pick the next migration number against all branches, not just HEAD.

7.3 Operating discipline

  • Long jobs go through the worker queue, never raw gcloud, so they are visible in the Training Cockpit.
  • Assume multi-step jobs fail on the first try. Build them batched and resumable up front, commit per item, and measure progress in the costly unit (bytes, rows), not item count.
  • Do not edit docs/PDR_PIPELINE_PLAN.md, its checklists, or the run catalog without being asked. Report results and wait.
  • Repository documents are the source of truth. Open the doc; do not infer from its title or a commit message.
  • Verify before defending. When Richard pushes back, that is context you lack. Dump the actual data or open the actual file first, then concede or push back with evidence. He does not want a yes-AI.

7.4 Scope

  • Mobile is maintained, not extended. New work needs no mobile view.
  • Issues live in GitHub, not in markdown and not in memory. Memory is for durable lessons; lifecycle state belongs in issues.
  • Richard handles the ethical and contractual calls. Surface a technical risk once, then defer.

8. The open frontier

Ordered by expected value to Maureen, not by how interesting the work is.

The executable sequence (2026-09-27). The issues that block the first end-to-end run carry the critical-path label, paired with p0. Their order, the decisions to fix first and the acceptance checks are in GH #439 and docs/mods_smoke_run_1_playbook.md. That run is a machinery test; items 2–4 below become its science follow-up.

Metro is a priority lane (Richard, 2026-10-05, D-Q4-04, GH #612). The Metro Map is the only Maple feature Maureen has asked about or researched. A useful Metro is the fastest way to get her using the system, and her use is the only user feedback this project will get.

  • Do as much with it as data and architecture allow.
  • Look for ways to bring new work into it, and its ideas into other surfaces.
  • Treat the metabolic hypotheses as research beside PDR, not a detour from it. The repurposing machinery can also be pointed at combinations of metabolic changes (diet, drugs or other), not only at single prescription drugs.

The lane is GH #595; the plan is docs/q4_2026_roadmap.md §4.5.

Tier 1 — directly on the critical path to G1

  1. Normalize and release her polygenic risk scores. PRS Gates D and F — the computation is done. This unblocks the disease half of the personalization loss, which has never seen a nonzero value.   2. Fix the edge-blind criterion (Phase 12, GH #47). Learn a signed per-relation strength so that sensitive_to pushes up and contraindication pushes down. No foundation retrain needed; each candidate is a fast criterion fine-tune. This is the most likely single cause of the null personalization result. Tier 2 — high value, larger scope
