1. Problem statement

What we have

PrimeKG and GP-KG operate at gene-level granularity. Variant-specific information exists in PrimeKG only as free text inside drug and disease description fields. Verified counts in docs/References/PrimeKG/:

  • drug_features.csv: 6 mentions of “V600” (in vemurafenib, cobimetinib, encorafenib, dabrafenib descriptions)
  • disease_features.csv: 4 mentions of “V600” (in BRAF-driven disease descriptions)
  • nodes.csv: zero variant-typed nodes — none of PrimeKG’s 10 node types is variant or mutation
  • edges.csv: zero variant-keyed edges — none of PrimeKG’s 30 relation types operates at variant granularity

What this costs us

The CompGCN+ConvE and ULTRA models we train see node IDs and edge types — not text. So variant signals from drug descriptions are invisible to the link-prediction model. Concretely, the graph cannot reason about:

  1. Mechanism-coupled drug selection. Maureen’s inherited variant raises a mechanism-coupled drug question (a nuanced call). The graph would need to traverse Maureen → variant → gene → pathway → drug class. Without variant nodes, the inference path collapses at the gene level.

  2. Combinatorial filtering. Maureen has KRAS / BRAF / MSI / MLH1 / MSH2 status from her Caris and RGCC reports, plus 73 expression genes and 224 drug sensitivities (112 drugs x 2 draws). Hand-coding a rule for every combination (“if BRAF wild-type AND KRAS wild-type AND MSS then EGFR inhibitors are eligible”) is brittle. Graph reasoning composes naturally.

  3. Subtype-specific drug sensitivity. PrimeKG knows “vemurafenib treats melanoma” but not “vemurafenib treats BRAF V600E-positive cancers.” With BRAF_V600E as a graph entity, the model could surface vemurafenib for any V600E-positive tumor, including CRC subtypes.

  4. Pharmacogenomic safety filtering. DPYD-deficient patients can have fatal toxicity to 5-FU and capecitabine. UGT1A1*28 homozygotes have severe irinotecan toxicity. Both are CRC chemotherapy concerns. Without variant-keyed contraindication edges, the graph ranks these drugs without regard to patient genotype — currently mitigated only by patient-side filtering, which is fragile.

Workarounds that exist (and what they recover)

  • Text embedders (Phase 7.6/7.7) pick up V600E and similar mentions from drug descriptions. They can recover variant-aware ranking via post-hoc combination, but only as good as the description text — sparse for non-cancer variants and absent for most PGx.
  • Patient-side filtering against mods_genomic_alteration can hard-filter or hard-promote at inference time. Real and useful, but ad hoc and doesn’t scale across patients or new evidence sources.
  • RAG / LLM re-ranking can layer variant context on top of the model’s top candidates. Limited because the LLM doesn’t see graph structure.

These together recover meaningful practical value, but they are workarounds. Variant-as-graph-entity is the principled architecture.

Why now

  • Phase 9 (Maureen personalization) becomes more powerful with variant nodes. Without them, every variant has to be hand-engineered into a scalar feature vector, losing all the public evidence linking variants to drugs.
  • Phase 12 (patient-as-node) makes the same gene-level mistake. Its DYSREGULATED_GENE patient→gene edges can’t distinguish BRAF V600E from BRAF V600K, even though the drug response differs.
  • The schema work (8.5.1, 8.5.2) is model-independent and can proceed in parallel with whatever PDR1/PDR2 training is happening.

Why not now

  • Engineering cost is real — see § 4 cost estimate. 6-18 weeks depending on path.
  • PrimeKG’s own authors didn’t include variants by design. We’re forking the philosophy as well as the data. Worth being deliberate about this.
  • Phase 11 (PDR2/ULTRA) makes 8.5.9 retraining cheaper. If PDR2 is on track, we may want to schedule the retrain step after Phase 11 closes.

2. Closure criteria (aggregate)

  1. Schema supports variant entity type and at least three new relation types covering predict-response, predict-toxicity, and variant-of-gene.
  2. All of Maureen’s parsed variants from mods_genomic_alteration are present as graph nodes with edges to the appropriate gene nodes.
  3. At least one public variant-evidence KB (OncoKB or CIViC for cancer; PharmGKB for PGx) is ingested and connected to the variant nodes.
  4. A retrained or fine-tuned model on the variant-extended KG meets two regression gates:
    • (a) Phase 7.5 RGCC validation numbers do not degrade.
    • (b) At least one validation track shows ≥ 0.03 AUROC or ≥ 0.05 Spearman ρ improvement.
  5. Documentation captures the identifier-normalization decisions, the merge methodology, and the architectural choice for retrain vs fine-tune.

8.5.8 — KG merge strategy and validation

  • Merge happens via shared identifier space, not via graph operations. Genes and drugs already in PrimeKG get their NCBI / DrugBank IDs reused; new variants get added with CAIDs; new edges connect them.
  • Audit cross-KB conflicts. When OncoKB says variant X predicts response to drug Y but CIViC says no evidence, do not collapse; preserve both as separate edges with provenance.
  • Run a sanity-check query before retraining: pick 10 well-known variant-drug pairs (BRAF V600E ↔ vemurafenib, DPYD c.1905+1G>A ↔ capecitabine, BRCA2 mutations ↔ olaparib, etc.). Verify each produces an edge with the expected provenance.
  • Document collision rates: how many variant nodes did we end up with? How many had ≥ 1 evidence edge from at least one source? How many were Maureen-relevant?

If Phase 11 has closed by the time we get here, Option B is much cheaper on PDR2 than on PDR1. 8.5.10 — Validation

  • Regression gate: re-run Phase 7.5 RGCC validation tiers on the variant-extended checkpoint. Numbers must not degrade. If they do, investigate before proceeding.
  • Lift gate: at least one validation metric improves measurably:
    • Tier 1 ρ (continuous correlation between model score and RGCC % cell death) improves by ≥ 0.05, OR
    • Tier 2 AUROC (high-sensitivity vs non-sensitive RGCC drugs) improves by ≥ 0.03
  • Maureen-specific anchor test: for each of her clinically actionable variants (ATM, KRAS / BRAF if present, MSI status, MLH1/MSH2, DPYD if tested, UGT1A1 if tested), verify the model now ranks the corresponding evidence-supported drugs in the top decile of CRC candidates. Pre-register the expected rankings before running.
  • Cross-validation on held-out variants: hold out 5% of OncoKB variant-drug edges; verify the model can predict them from the rest of the graph. This is the link-prediction analog of leave-one-out.

4. KG merging in practice — context for the planning session

The Phase 8.5 plan above understates how hard KG merging is. Adding rows to tables is the trivial 20%. The hard 80% is identifier alignment, schema harmonization, provenance tracking, and update lifecycle. This section captures the field’s state of the art so we can choose the right tooling.

4.1 The four hard problems

(1) Identifier alignment. Every database uses different IDs for the same entities. PrimeKG uses NCBI gene IDs (9796 = PHYHIP), OncoKB uses HGNC symbols (BRAF), CIViC uses Entrez IDs, ClinVar uses gene symbols + RefSeq. For drugs it’s worse: DrugBank, ChEMBL, PubChem CID, RxNorm, NCIt, KEGG, ATC — and the same drug routinely has different IDs in different sources. Variants are the worst case: HGVS notation has multiple equivalent forms, dbSNP IDs change between builds, ClinGen’s CAID is canonical but coverage is incomplete.

(2) Schema harmonization. PrimeKG is a binary KG: every fact is a (head, relation, tail) triple. OncoKB has hyperedges with metadata: (variant, drug, evidence_level, indication_context, citation, level_of_evidence). To merge, you either (a) flatten into multiple triples and lose information, or (b) extend the schema with edge attributes. Both have downsides.

(3) Provenance and conflict resolution. When two sources disagree (OncoKB says Level 2 evidence, CIViC says no evidence), which wins? Best practice: preserve both, mark each with source, and let downstream consumers decide. But this means every node and edge needs a provenance attribute, which most KGs don’t have natively.

(4) Update lifecycle. OncoKB updates monthly. CIViC has continuous community curation. PrimeKG is a single-version dataset release. Synchronizing updates across heterogeneous cadences is a real engineering problem — usually solved by re-deriving the merged KG on a schedule rather than incremental updates.

4.2 The Biolink + NCATS Translator ecosystem (the canonical answer in our space)

This is the closest thing to a solved problem for biomedical KG integration:

  • Biolink Model — a controlled hierarchy of node and edge types, with explicit semantics. e.g., biolink:Gene, biolink:Drug, biolink:Variant, biolink:treats, biolink:contraindicated_for. Every concept maps to a Biolink class.
  • TRAPI (Translator Reasoner API) — a JSON-RPC standard for querying KGs. Each Knowledge Provider exposes their KB through TRAPI, regardless of internal storage.
  • SRI Node Normalizer — a hosted REST service that maps any biomedical identifier (NCBI Gene, HGNC, DrugBank, MONDO, UMLS, etc.) to a canonical CURIE plus a Biolink class. This is the answer to identifier alignment for ~95% of biomedical entities.
  • NCATS Translator itself — operates 13+ Knowledge Providers (RTX-KG2, ROBOKOP, Multi-Omics Provider, etc.) behind a unified TRAPI layer. We already have a connector at backend/connectors/translator_connector.py.

Practical implication: instead of building four bespoke connectors for OncoKB / CIViC / PharmGKB / ClinVar, much of the variant-evidence content is already accessible through Translator’s MolePro and BTE Knowledge Providers. We’d query Translator with normalized variant CURIEs and pull back evidence edges already harmonized to Biolink. Where coverage gaps exist (OncoKB specifically isn’t in Translator due to licensing), we build direct connectors.

  10. Maureen-specific scope. ✅ RESOLVED — 19 variants total is small enough to ingest everything. No need to triage to an “actionable subset.” All her variants from all reports go in.
