PrimeKG merged nodes: review table for #438 item 2

Refs #438 item 2. Richard decided on 2026-10-05 to “fix all the wrong merges”, starting from this table. Related: roadmap D13 (#611), the Jaccard safety report (#59). The fix lands before Run 3’s export.

Decided 2026-10-05: D-Q4-22 A (#819). Rules J0–J9 were approved as proposed, and the six unclear groups split along the CSV’s partition column, as the review leans. kg_split_primekg_merges.py carries it out (#832).

With that partition the split restores 109,412 rows, not the 109,440 quoted below. §4’s ”+ the 6 unclear” column splits D-glucose from dextrose, and J5 keeps those two together. The difference is 28 enzyme and transporter rows. Fed the approved partition, the replay in §8.5 gives the same 109,412.

Nothing here changes data. Whether two nodes are one entity is a mapping decision, so under the CLAUDE.md hard rule this table goes to Richard before any entity is split. Each verdict is a proposal. Where a verdict rests on a rule rather than on evidence, the rule is named (J0–J9, §3), and those rules are what he approves. Counts were read from prod, read-only and PrimeKG rows only, at 15:06 PDT on 2026-10-05, and replayed from PrimeKG’s own kg.csv.

Files. This page, and kg_primekg_merge_review.csv beside it: one row per merged node (401 rows), with every measured column and the proposal. §8 has the two scripts that produce it.

Summary

  • 401 PrimeKG nodes sit on 183 canonical entities. The audit’s “218” is the surplus: 401 − 183 = 218 nodes that lost an entity of their own. By node type:
    • drug: 237 nodes in 101 groups, a surplus of 136 (the audit’s “136”);
    • gene: 122 nodes in 61 groups;
    • pathway: 36 nodes in 18 groups;
    • anatomy: 4 nodes in 2 groups;
    • disease: 2 nodes in 1 group.
  • The audit’s edge counts reproduce exactly. Replaying kg.csv under these merges gives 7,988,012 relations. That is what prod stores, on every one of the 33 relation keys and on every one of the 183 entities. The merges collapsed 112,486 rows: drug_drug 108,174, contraindication 1,566, indication 428, and nine smaller relations (§1).
  • Three mechanisms made the groups (§2).
    • 63 groups (61 gene, 2 anatomy): PrimeKG ships two nodes for one id. Either two symbols of one gene (C16orf58 and RUSF1, both NCBI Gene 64755) or two labels of one UBERON term. These were one entity from the first backfill. The merges are right and cost no rows.
    • 101 groups (all drugs): Node Normalizer gave their DrugBank ids one preferred CURIE. apply_canonical_entity_merges.py pass 1 then merged them in May 2026. Node Normalizer folds salts, esters, enantiomers and radiotracers into one chemical, and twice it folded together unrelated drugs.
    • 19 groups (18 pathway, 1 disease): the names matched, and the same script’s pass 2 merged them. Ten of them join a SARS-CoV-1 pathway to its SARS-CoV-2 namesake.
  • Proposed verdicts: 110 distinct, 67 same, 6 unclear.
    • Same (67): the 63 one-id groups, two hydrate or “unspecified form” pairs (J5), and two pairs that are one product under two DrugBank entries (J8): CT-011 with pidilizumab, and PEG-uricase with pegloticase.
    • Distinct (110): 91 drug groups, all 18 pathway groups and the disease group. 29 are distinct on evidence alone: unrelated drugs, different molecules, different viruses. The other 81 rest on one of J1–J4, J6, J7 or J9.
    • Unclear (6): drug groups where the evidence does not settle it (§3).
    • Both known examples are distinct. D- and L-tartaric acid are enantiomers (J1). Choline C-11 and choline salicylate sit in a group of four different drugs.
  • What a split restores. Splitting the 110 distinct groups restores 106,818 rows. That includes every lost contraindication (1,566), indication (428), drug_effect (888) and off-label use (46) row. Splitting the 6 unclear groups as well restores 109,440. The remaining 3,046 rows stay merged on purpose. They sit in the same groups and in the two kept pairs of the sodium phosphate group, where each is one assertion made twice (§4).
  • What Richard approves: the ten rules in §3. Each comes with the number of groups it decides. A rejected rule flips its own groups and no others, and the CSV names the rule behind every node.

The six unclear groups. The CSV’s partition column gives the split I lean to for each.

  • Calcium phosphate and its dihydrate. DrugBank’s “Calcium Phosphate” does not say which calcium phosphate it is. The dihydrate (MW 172.09) is dicalcium phosphate.
  • D-glucose, dextrose, and beta-D-glucose. J5 merges the first two, which carry the same DrugBank text. Beta-D-glucose is an anomer, and anomers interconvert in water.
  • Dihydroergocryptine and dihydro-alpha-ergocryptine. DrugBank’s own text calls the alpha form “usually … the mixture”.
  • Camphor and synthetic camphor. Neither entry states its stereochemistry.
  • Two influenza vaccine antigen groups (A/California/7/2009 X-181, two entries; B/Brisbane/60/2008, four entries). Each group’s entries differ in antigen type or inactivation method and have identical PrimeKG edges.

A caution that cuts both ways. In 38 drug groups, PrimeKG lists drug–drug interactions between two members of the same group: 82 pairs, such as estradiol with each of its esters. The merges deleted those rows as self-loops. For a distinct group they are real assertions, and a split restores them. Three same groups have them too: CT-011 with pidilizumab, magnesium acetate with its tetrahydrate, and PEG-uricase with pegloticase. DrugBank generates interactions by drug class, so there they are a product interacting with itself, and they stay deleted. An interaction between two entries proves two DrugBank entries, not two drugs.

5. Surprises

  1. Node Normalizer conflates unrelated drugs. Guanabenz, an alpha-2 agonist, shares an entity with icodextrin, a glucose polymer for peritoneal dialysis. Ethanol shares one with ipecac, a plant emetic. Its cliques also join different molecules: human insulin with four analogues and beef insulin, three Tc-99m agents that share only the radionuclide, and Lu-177 dotatate (therapy) with Ga-68 dotatate (imaging).
  2. SARS-CoV-1 and SARS-CoV-2 pathways are merged. Reactome builds both virus hierarchies with the same pathway names. Pass 2 merged ten such pairs by name, along with 8 disease-pathway pairs that share a name.
  3. One entity’s CURIE names a third antigen. Entity 88504 carries DRUGBANK:DB14432, an A/Singapore/gp1908/2015 IVR-180 antigen. Its members are two A/California/7/2009 X-181 antigens, and PrimeKG has no node for DB14432. The merge script gave each surviving entity Node Normalizer’s preferred CURIE.
  4. Name and CURIE disagree on 47 of the 101 drug entities. The name comes from the member with the most edges; the CURIE comes from Node Normalizer. Sometimes they name different substances:
    • “Choline salicylate” carries CHEBI:15354, choline;
    • “Procaine benzylpenicillin” carries CHEBI:17334, penicillin;
    • “Technetium Tc-99m exametazime” carries CHEBI:33353, the technetium atom;
    • “Levonorgestrel” carries CHEBI:231594, norgestrel.
  5. Rows lost understate the harm. 16 drug groups lost no row to their own merge. They include both tartaric acids, guanabenz with icodextrin, and ketoconazole with levoketoconazole. Each still serves two drugs’ edges as one node, and a split is what fixes that.
  6. A re-run would merge them again. For 228 of the 237 drug members, mods_kg_curie_cache maps the standard DRUGBANK: spelling of the id to the merged entity’s CURIE. Pass 1 groups on exactly that. A split therefore needs a guard, or the next run of the script, or a fresh backfill with Node Normalizer on, will merge them again. Pass 2 would re-merge the pathway pairs by name.
  7. A PrimeKG mapping question sits behind two correct gene merges. The node PrimeKG names “COX2” (NCBI Gene 4513, MT-CO2) has disease edges to colorectal cancer, colorectal carcinoma and colorectal neoplasm, beside MELAS and cytochrome-c oxidase deficiency. The colorectal ones look like PTGS2 (COX-2) associations filed under MT-CO2 by symbol, the COX2 homonym of #752. “COX1” (MT-CO1) shows the same pattern. The merges are right, since each pair shares one id, and a split would not touch these edges. They are worth their own check, because colorectal cancer is the index disease.

6. Before any split: open points for the follow-up

These are notes for whoever implements the approved splits. None is decided here.

  • Other sources’ rows on these entities were not measured. This review read PrimeKG rows only. Node Normalizer answers for an id, not for a source, so any source that met one of these DrugBank ids sits on the merged entity too. The split must route those rows as well.
  • The patient overlay. Maureen’s drug objects that resolved to one of these entities need re-checking, and materialize_patient_kg() must run after the split. The overlay was out of scope here: no maple_patient row was read.
    • One check needed no patient data. Paclitaxel, one of the RGCC taxanes the mission spec names, is merged with paclitaxel poliglumex. Every poliglumex row duplicated a paclitaxel row or joined the two, so the paclitaxel node carries no foreign edge today.
  • The re-merge guard (surprise 6): pass 1 and pass 2 of apply_canonical_entity_merges.py, and the cache rows behind them.
  • Which member keeps the entity row. A natural choice is the member the entity’s CURIE names, or the parent compound; each other member gets its own entity under its standard CURIE. First check whether an entity with that CURIE already exists. Entity 88504’s CURIE has to change whichever member keeps it.
  • Expected counts. For any approved set of partitions, primekg_merge_review.py replays the expected rows restored by relation (§8). The proposal as written gives 106,818.
  • Backup, dry run, expected count, per the plan in #438’s 2026-10-05 comment.

  • Prod, read-only. The snapshot ran in the backend container, against the production database. Every session opened with SET SESSION CHARACTERISTICS AS TRANSACTION READ ONLY, and the script asserts transaction_read_only = on.

    • Nothing patient-side was read: no maple_patient row, no mods_patient, no document or EHR table.
    • The snapshot was taken on 2026-10-05 at 15:06 PDT and came to 243 KB.
  • PrimeKG kg.csv.

    • SHA-256 0a5a010fcd88dcec572a573d98c26c1c244a11567092b6161d3ded611cfb5c69.
    • The bundle copy at C:\Maple\docs\References\PrimeKG\Data\kg.csv (downloaded 2026-04-09) is byte-identical.
  • Evidence for the verdicts, cited in the reason and basis columns:
    • DrugBank descriptions, groups and molecular weights from PrimeKG’s drug_features.csv;
    • MONDO names and BERT groups from disease_features.csv;

8.2 Reproduce

Save §8.4 and §8.5 as files, then, from Git Bash on the host:

On 2026-10-05, step 2 run against the 15:06 PDT snapshot reproduced the committed CSV byte for byte. The script writes LF line endings, and a Windows checkout with core.autocrlf on holds CRLF, so compare with diff --strip-trailing-cr. Its console line reports two checks, and both held:

  • the stored relations match the replay on all 33 relation keys;
  • they match on all 183 entities, relation by relation.

A later snapshot gives the same table until the store changes.

The proposal columns are written by hand: verdict, partition, rules, reason and basis. The script only carries them over, by group key. The key is the group’s PrimeKG x_index values joined by +, which do not change between environments. A partition lists the proposed nodes after a split: blocks separated by |, members of a block joined by +. To test another proposal, edit those columns and re-run step 2. The JSON’s scenarios then gives the rows each split restores, by relation.
