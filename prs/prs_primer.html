1. What a polygenic score is

1.1 The arithmetic

A polygenic score is a weighted sum, and that is genuinely all it is:

PRS = Σ  dosageᵢ × weightᵢ

For each of a set of positions in the genome, dosage is how many copies of the trait-associated allele this person carries — 0, 1, or 2, because we have two copies of each autosome — and weight is how much that allele shifted the trait in a large association study. Multiply, add, done. A thousand multiplications and one sum.

The entire difficulty of the field lives in three places, none of which is the arithmetic:

  1. Getting the weights. That is the GWAS literature’s job, and it is upstream of us.
  2. Lining the two sides up. Deciding that this row in a scoring file and this position in this person’s genome refer to the same thing, on the same strand, with the same allele playing the same role. This is where essentially every bug we have hit actually lived.
  3. Interpreting the total. A raw sum is a number with no units and no meaning until it is placed against a reference distribution of people (§1.4).

The through-line: the idea (Fisher) is a century old, the data (GWAS) is twenty years old, the practical method (ISC) is fifteen, and the standardized plumbing (PGS Catalog) is five. We are working entirely in that last layer.

1.3 What a polygenic score is not

Worth stating plainly, because the failure modes of this pipeline are mostly failures of interpretation rather than of code:

  • Not a diagnosis, and not a probability. A raw PRS is an unnormalized sum. On its own it is meaningless — it only acquires meaning relative to a population.
  • Not portable across ancestries without care. See 2017–2019 above.
  • Not the whole genetic story. A PRS captures common, additive liability. It says nothing about a rare high-penetrance variant. A rare high-penetrance germline variant is exactly the kind of thing a PRS structurally cannot see — which is why the variant-level pipeline and the PRS pipeline are separate inputs to the same model.
  • Not causal. Weights sit on tag variants in LD with whatever is actually doing the work.
  • Not comparable between scores. Two scores for the same trait, from different studies with different weight conventions, produce numbers on different scales.

1.4 Why Maple computes them

This matters for design, because our use is not the usual one. MODS does not compute PRS in order to hand somebody a clinical risk report. It computes them as a broad, patient-specific genetic feature vector that feeds the PDR2 personalized loss:

L = L_pred − λ₁·L_prs − λ₂·L_expr

where L_prs aggregates, over diseases i, a learnable weight wᵢ times the patient’s PRSᵢ times the model’s per-triple score. The polygenic score for each trait becomes a signed, learnable nudge on drug–disease edges incident to that disease node — the patient enters the objective, not just the inputs.

Take the shape, not the algebra. The paper’s literal formula (L_prs = Σᵢ wᵢ·PRSᵢ·s_pred) is unbounded as written, and the released code adds tanh(·) and L2 weight normalization that the text omits. We verified those nonlinearities are load-bearing. losses.py is the operational specification — see `pdr2_loss_text_vs_code_note.md` and `pdr_personalization_writeup.md`.

Three consequences follow, and they explain choices that would otherwise look strange:

  1. Breadth beats depth. We want as many scores as the genome supports, not a curated clinical shortlist. A wide feature vector is the point. This is why the target is the ~5,377-score catalog and not thirteen diseases.

    The trap this is guarding against, stated bluntly (Richard, 2026-09-23, after a session report led with “44 colorectal scores are eligible”): counting disease-relevant scores is not a result. We already know she has colorectal cancer, and we know a great deal about it. A colorectal PRS can, at best, tell us whether she was unlucky in getting it — which changes no decision. No individual score and no individual disease is the object of interest. These are ML inputs. The goal is to maximise the number of usable data points without regard to what they predict; anything that refines that data is a direction to move toward, and anything that degrades it a direction to move away from. When tempted to report “N scores for disease X”, report the size and quality of the feature vector instead.

  2. The relative ordering across a cohort matters more than any absolute number — but we have a cohort of one, so a reference distribution is required to make the value mean anything at all. That is Gate D.

  3. A wrong number is worse than a missing number. A missing feature is a zero the loss ignores. A wrong feature is a confident, learnable lie. Every “we defer this rather than approximate it” decision below traces back to this line.

A corollary that changes how the partial-coverage decision should be made — and a correction to how this document used to justify it. A score computed on only part of its loci is not simply “wrong” the way it would be clinically, so the coverage bar for “usable as an ML feature” sits genuinely lower than for “usable as a clinical risk estimate.” That conclusion stands. The reason previously given for it did not.

Superseded (2026-09-23). This section used to argue that a roughly constant deflation is “largely absorbable by wᵢ — the model can rescale a feature.” The released loss cannot do that. losses.py L2-normalizes the weight vector before the inner product and then squashes the result:

‖ŵ‖₂ = 1 is a hard constraint, so the model cannot rescale a feature — it can only re-allocate a fixed unit budget across diseases. Feature scale therefore is not absorbed; it decides the answer.

What actually absorbs a constant deflation is Gate D, not the model. A percentile (or a z-score) is computed against a reference distribution, and a multiplicative deflation applied equally to the patient and the panel is a monotone transform — it preserves rank exactly, so the normalised feature is unchanged. The absorption is in the normalisation, which is another reason Gate D is not optional (§5.5).

Three things keep it from being free, and the first is now the mechanism rather than a caveat:

  • The deflation must be consistent between the patient and the reference distribution (Gate D). Coverage that differs between Maureen and the panel biases the percentile, and nothing downstream can undo a bias introduced in normalisation. Score the panel through the same Gate C policy or this guarantee evaporates.
  • It is not a pure scale: which loci are missing matters, and a few large-weight loci can deflate a score disproportionately, which breaks the exact rank preservation above. Over many loci it approximates one.
  • Fewer loci means a noisier estimate of the same liability. That is variance, not bias — and variance is far more tolerable in a feature than bias is.

The corresponding trap on the other side: raw sums are not a usable feature vector. Across the 2,322 production-eligible scores the theoretical magnitude bound max(|lo|, |hi|) spans roughly twelve orders of magnitude — median 15.9, p95 706, max 2.59 × 10⁷, with 1,818 of 2,322 (78 %) already above 3. Under tanh(⟨ŵ, PRS⟩) with ‖ŵ‖₂ = 1, a vector on those scales saturates the squash, 1 − tanh² ≈ 0, and the gradient dies. Track A (all-zero PRS) produced no gradient because tanh(0) = 0; feeding raw sums would produce no gradient from the opposite end, and the “interpretable” disease weights would converge to a readout of which published score happens to carry the largest raw magnitude rather than which disease matters. Normalised features are what put ⟨ŵ, PRS⟩ back inside tanh’s live band.

The operational consequence: unplaceable_rows should not be a binary promote/defer flag. It should be a coverage threshold, chosen empirically by what it does to the feature vector. The instrument for choosing it already exists — the coverage_v1 payload’s third quantity (effective variance-weighted information, §5.4) is exactly the right statistic, and the dual-scorer reconciliation (§5.4) separates the imputation term, which measures precisely how much a partially-covered score changes when the missing dosages are filled in. Run that on the affected scores and the threshold stops being a judgement call.

2. Reference genomes

Every coordinate in this pipeline — in the patient’s gVCF, in a scoring file, in dbSNP — is meaningless except relative to a specific reference genome build. Mixing builds is the single easiest way to produce a plausible, entirely wrong score.

2.1 What the reference actually is

The human reference genome is not any one person’s genome. It is a curated haploid consensus assembled from a handful of anonymous donors (with one donor, RP11, contributing a majority), maintained by the Genome Reference Consortium. It carries the reference allele at every position by convention, not by biological privilege — at plenty of loci the “reference” base is the minor allele in most populations.

This matters directly for us: §5.1’s bug is precisely a case where being reference-matching is not the same as being effect-free.

2.3 Naming and assembly flavors

Two orthogonal traps:

Contig naming. UCSC writes chr1, chrX, chrM. Ensembl and the GRC write 1, X, MT. NCBI writes accessions (NC_000001.11). These name identical sequence. Every entry point in our code normalizes through connectors/reference_fasta.py::normalize_chrom (strip a leading chr), and we standardize on the Ensembl convention because that is what the patient’s gVCF and the PGS Catalog’s hm_chr column both use.

Assembly flavor. “GRCh38” can mean the primary assembly (chromosomes + unplaced scaffolds), the full analysis set (primary + alt contigs + decoys), or a no-alt subset. Alt contigs are alternate versions of polymorphic regions; if reads can align to both an alt and its primary counterpart, mapping quality collapses and variant calls quietly degrade. We use the primary assembly — 194 contigs — which matches how the gVCF was called.

2.4 Liftover, and why we do not do it at score time

Liftover converts coordinates between builds using a chain file of mapped blocks. It is lossy: some regions have no equivalent, some invert, some map many-to-one. The GRCh37→GRCh38 shift is large and irregular — on PGS000006, only 3 of 313 loci kept the same coordinate, with shifts up to 2.87 Mb.

Our position is that liftover is the PGS Catalog’s job, not ours. We download only the _hmPOS_GRCh38 harmonized files, so every scoring file arrives already on our build. The patient’s gVCF was called on GRCh38. No conversion happens anywhere in the scoring path, which removes an entire class of error.

A blank hm_pos on a score authored against another assembly is now an unplaceable row, which is what it always meant. Gate C counts those rows as unplaceable_row and they never enter a sum.

3. Scoring files

3.1 Where they come from

The PGS Catalog (EMBL-EBI + University of Cambridge) is the standardized public repository of published polygenic scores. Each score gets a stable PGS###### accession, structured metadata (trait, EFO/MONDO mapping, citation, training cohort, ancestry), and a downloadable scoring file.

Four things are visible in three rows, and each has cost us something:

  1. chr_position ≠ hm_pos. 100880328 vs 100414772 — a 466 kb shift from the GRCh37→GRCh38 harmonization. Reading the wrong column is a silent catastrophe.
  2. hm_rsID is blank — and not just in these rows: all 313 of them. This score identifies its variants by position only. A pipeline that joins on rsID matches literally zero rows here and reports a confident zero.
  3. Row 3 is an indel — C → CAAA, an insertion. Note the effect allele is the longer one; the shared anchor base C is what makes indel orientation unresolvable from the reference alone (§5.2).
  4. allelefrequency_effect is present. This is the field that lets a scorer resolve a strand-ambiguous palindromic SNV — if the effect allele frequency is far from 0.5, the strand can be inferred.

Optional columns that change everything. Beyond the core schema, a scoring file may carry is_haplotype, is_diplotype, is_interaction, is_dominant, is_recessive, dosage_0_weight/dosage_1_weight/dosage_2_weight, variant_description, locus_name, OR, HR. Any of these means the file is not a plain additive sum, and quietly summing effect_weight over such a file produces a number that looks fine and means nothing. Detecting them is the classifier’s first job (§4).

Here is what one of those looks like — PGS004262, a GenoBoost psoriasis score:

chr_name  chr_position  effect_allele  other_allele  dosage_0_weight  dosage_1_weight  dosage_2_weight  ...
6         31274555      T              C            -0.0324954        0.1051552        0.2502743

There is no effect_weight column at all. The weights are per-genotype, and they are not linear in dosage (−0.032 → +0.105 → +0.250). An effect_weight-based parser reads zero rows from this file. Fifteen catalog scores are exactly this case, and the right conclusion is “needs a dedicated calculator,” not “the file is broken” — a distinction §4.4 encodes deliberately.

The catalog is not uniform. It is two populations of file wearing one format.

Those two rankings differ, which is a small but recurring trap: sizing work by file count, by bytes, and by rows gives three different answers. Across all 5,377 files the classifier examined 3,452,540,960 variant rows.

The split is historical, not arbitrary: pre-2015 scores are curated lists of genome-wide significant hits (tens to hundreds of variants, rsID-labelled), and post-2015 scores are LD-aware genome-wide posteriors (millions of variants, position-only). Any assumption that holds for one population fails for the other — which is why a 25-score smoke test once suggested weight_type was uniformly NR, a conclusion the full fleet demolished.

Cost model (measured, after optimization): ~0.98 s per MB of gzip → ~16.7 CPU-hours for the whole catalog. Sharded six ways on a 16-logical-core box that is ~2.9 h wall. Two non-obvious facts from profiling a 19.4 MB / 972k-row file:

  • The time is not I/O. gzip decompression plus readlines is 6 %. Tab-splitting is 8 %. Per-row dict construction plus PGSVariant allocation is 45 %, and feature accumulation another 41 %. The lever is Python object allocation, not decompression.
  • Therefore: shard by bytes, not by file count, and compute ETA from bytes. Under largest-first ordering, byte-progress and count-progress diverge wildly — at 1.2 % of scores the fleet run was already 12.1 % of bytes.

Cache integrity. Five of the 5,377 cached files were truncated by interrupted downloads (PGS002938, PGS002153, PGS000257, PGS003724, PGS004604); re-downloads were 25–62 % larger. A truncated gzip always fails its CRC/length trailer, so EOFError: Compressed file ended before the end-of-stream marker detects this reliably — a partial file can never be silently parsed as complete. Do not assume a cached scoring file is intact merely because it exists.

3.4 Weights and weight types

A weight is the per-allele effect size the study estimated. What scale it is on is declared (sometimes) in the #weight_type= header, and getting this wrong is a biological error the arithmetic cannot catch.

The scales you should expect:

Weight typeMeaningAdditive-summable?
beta (β)Effect on a continuous trait, in the trait’s own units (or SD units).✅ Yes — this is the natural additive scale.
log(OR), ln(OR)Natural log of the odds ratio, for a binary disease.✅ Yes. Logs add; that is the point of taking them.

The theoretical-bounds check. For any additive score, the raw total is mathematically bounded:

L = Σ min(0, 2·wᵢ)        U = Σ max(0, 2·wᵢ)

because every dosage is in {0, 1, 2}. Every computed score must satisfy L ≤ S ≤ U; a value outside that interval is a scorer defect, not a biological finding. It is free, it is computed for every score at classify time (stored as theoretical_lo/_hi), and it catches whole families of allele-orientation and arithmetic bugs.

It is also one-sided and scale-blind, which is worth internalizing: it cannot detect an odds-ratio-summed-as-a-beta, and it cannot detect a wrong-but-attainable score. We learned that concretely — a bounds check over the 40 legacy scores with |raw| > 1000 found all 40 inside their bounds, while every one of them was in fact wrong (§5.1). Bounds are a floor on correctness, not a ceiling.

3.6 The classification engine

Source: backend/mods_prs_compat.py · Tests: backend/tests/test_mods_prs_compat.py · Runner: worker task mods_prs_manifest_build, driven by backend/scripts/prs/build_prs_manifest.py.

Five design properties are worth understanding, because each was paid for:

1. One parser, one accumulator. There is exactly one row parser (pgs_catalog_connector._row_to_variant) and exactly one feature implementation (mods_prs_compat._FeatureAccumulator). Both the in-memory path (parse_scoring_file → score_features) and the streaming path (open_scoring_file → stream_score_features) drive that same accumulator, and both classifications land in the same classify_features. This is not tidiness — these counts decide production_eligible, so if the two read paths could disagree about a score’s features, they could disagree about whether it is safe to compute. Do not “optimize” by forking a second parse loop.

2. Conservative by presence. For complex-format and inheritance columns, the classifier flags on the presence of the column, not on confirming that rows use it. This over-defers a score carrying an unused column — the safe direction. Row-level refinement is a planned hardening step, not a bug.

3. Precedence is load-bearing. The order is D (unplaceable) > C (dedicated) > D (no usable rows) > B > A, and the placement of the second D check after C is deliberate: a dosage_N_weight file parses to zero effect_weight rows, and “needs a dedicated calculator” is the true explanation where “no usable rows” would falsely imply a broken file. Fifteen catalog scores hit exactly this path. Equally, that check must come before B and A — under classifier v1 an empty file had every feature count at zero, fell through to Class A, and was marked production-eligible: a score cleared for production that computes to nothing.

4. Versioned decisions. Every manifest row carries classifier_version and the scoring file’s sha256. A change in classifier meaning — or in what it extracts — bumps the version so a re-run re-classifies rather than skipping rows decided under different rules. v3 was the 2026-09-22 structural re-classify, v4 the unplaceable_rows threshold promotion (recomputed in place, no re-read), and v5 (2026-09-26, GH #377) adds the allele-convention counters below and therefore needs a full re-read.

A bump that is meant to change no decision should be made to prove it: scripts/prs/verify_classifier_bump.py snapshot before the re-run and compare after it diff every score’s class, categories and eligibility. v5 moves no counter and promotes no category, so its diff must be empty — and an empty diff is also how you know the re-run actually ran.

4b. The allele-convention profile (v5). n_snv_pair_top (A/C + A/G) and n_snv_pair_bot (C/T + G/T) split a file’s non-palindromic biallelic SNVs in two. On the forward strand those are the same variants seen from the two strands and occur about equally often, so top_fraction = top/(top+bot) sits at ~0.5 — measured 0.4991, 0.5017, 0.5018 on three ordinary scores. ~1.0 or ~0.0 means the alleles were written in an A/B convention (Illumina TOP or BOT), whose strand assignment is made per variant and recorded nowhere in the file, so about half its rows are reverse-complemented relative to GRCh38 with nothing saying which half.

Counts, never the ratio — mods_prs_compat.top_fraction derives it on read, the same rule Gate C’s coverage payload follows. And the profile alone never condemns a file: PGS005087/5088/5089 are top_fraction = 1.000 with a 0 % complement-only rate, their alleles being the forward-strand pair at 5,326 of 5,326 checkable loci. The guard needs the measured complement-only rate too (§5.4). Full reasoning: `prs_validation/palindrome_orientation_spike.md`.

5. Memory discipline at catalog scale. parse_scoring_file holds the decompressed text, the split-line list, and every PGSVariant at once — fine for one file, fatal across 59 GB. The streaming reader yields lazily. Two further optimizations, each verified behavior-identical against a verbatim reimplementation across 16 real files (72 → 1.1 M variants, classes A/B/C, zero mismatches):

  • _ColumnIndex resolves column positions once per file, so _row_to_variant indexes the split fields rather than building a dict per row — a ~1.3× speedup.
  • Allele pairs are interned to small integers before per-site storage. A scoring file has a handful of distinct allele pairs but millions of sites; keeping the pair strings per site dominated peak memory at 1,040 MB on the 9.5 M-row file. Interning is exact — distinct pairs get distinct ids — so unlike hashing it cannot under-count multiallelic sites, which would silently promote a score to eligible.

Cross-validation is still open. The classifier is deliberately structural and dependency-free, which means it is our reading of the format rather than the reference implementation’s. GH #237 tracks cross-validating it against the official pgscatalog libraries.

5. The processing engine

5.1 The bug that defines the design

Everything below is shaped by one defect, found by external audit on 2026-07-12, that invalidated all 5,368 previously computed scores.

The old genotype store was variant-only: the VCF importer skipped reference blocks, so a position where the patient matches the reference has no row. The scorer then did the obvious thing — a scoring locus not found in the store was counted as missing and contributed nothing.

That is correct only when the effect allele is the alternate allele. When the PGS effect allele is the reference allele — and a person who is homozygous-reference therefore carries two copies of the effect allele — the correct dosage is 2 and the correct contribution is 2 × weight. The pipeline scored 0.

Measured on PGS000019, ~49 % of loci are REF-effect (25 REF vs 26 ALT). At those loci the dosage map became {0, 1, 0} instead of {2, 1, 0} — an “artificial heterozygote.” This is a bias, not a constant offset: it distorts different scores by different amounts depending on how many of their effect alleles happen to be reference.

Three lessons were extracted, and they are worth more than the fix:

  • “Absent” is ambiguous in a variant-only store. It can mean hom-ref or not sequenced, and those must never collapse to the same number.
  • snps_used / snps_in_scoring_file is not coverage. That ratio clustered at 42–52 % across the legacy fleet, and the retired UI colour-coded it as a data-quality verdict about the published score — presenting a symptom of our own defect as somebody else’s problem.
  • A self-check that shares your assumptions cannot fail. The internal reconstruction that “confirmed” the ~50 % coverage was benign assumed absent = dosage 0, so it reproduced the bug instead of detecting it. The audit caught it by asking the one question the check could not.

The response was to wipe rather than quarantine: 9,793 rows deleted on 2026-07-13 (5,368 distinct scores plus superseded re-runs), backed up first to backend/data/prs_backups/. Scores are fully recomputable from the retained genotypes plus the scoring files, so keeping known-wrong numbers around bought nothing and risked a leak. The 8,088,434-row mods_genotype table was untouched.

5.2 Dense callsets

Source: backend/connectors/wgs_dense_callset.py · Locus union: backend/connectors/pgs_locus_union.py · Worker task: genotype_dense_callset

The fix is to stop treating “absent” as “0” by making absence impossible at the sites we care about. The mechanism is a dense callset: a genotype record for every locus some target score needs, whether or not the patient carries a variant there.

“Dense” does not mean all 3.1 billion positions. It means dense over the union of PGS loci — a site-targeted reconstruction, typically a few million positions, built from the source gVCF rather than from the variant-only database. This is deliberate: build_locus_union() collects the (chrom, pos, alleles) sites the target scores actually reference, deduplicated, and the builder resolves exactly those.

Every target locus resolves to exactly one of four outcomes:

OutcomeConditionEmitted
CALLABLE_HOMREFA confident reference block spans the coordinate.GT 0/0 with a concrete REF (from the FASTA) and the scoring non-ref ALT — so a REF-effect locus becomes dosage-2 eligible.
CALLABLE_VARIANTAn explicit, quality-passing variant record.Its genotype.
EXPLICIT_MISSINGA no-call, a FAILed record, below-threshold quality, or no record at all../. — routed to the missingness policy, never silently dropped.
STRUCTURAL_FAILREF disagrees with the FASTA, contig absent, orientation unresolvable.Nothing. Logged, excluded, never a partial score.

And the Gate A exit invariant is a hard assertion, not a log line:

callable_homref + callable_variant + explicit_missing + structural_fail == union_size

i.e. zero residual implicit-zero. Every locus is accounted for by name. If the arithmetic does not reconcile, the build raises rather than returning a plausible result.

The callability policy, and the discovery that nearly re-introduced the bug. The patient’s gVCF is Sequencing.com 30× WGS, reblocked by GvcfReblocker — GATK-style (END/MIN_DP reference blocks, not DRAGEN RGQ), Ensembl contig naming. The provisional plan said hom-ref calls should require GQ ≥ 20, which is standard advice. Measuring the real file showed every PASS reference block carries GQ = 0 — 0 of 159,540 in the first 300k records had GQ > 0, because the reblocker does not preserve per-site GQ across collapsed blocks. Gating hom-ref on GQ would have rejected every hom-ref call and silently re-created the exact defect being fixed.

So the policy identifies the caller from the header first, then applies thresholds:

Rule
hom-ref callableref block spans coord, FILTER == PASS, GT == 0/0, MIN_DP ≥ 10, DP ≤ 3 × autosomal median, REF verified against the FASTA. No GQ gate.
variant callableFILTER == PASS, GQ ≥ 20, DP ≥ 10, REF == FASTA. Indels stricter: DP ≥ 15, GQ ≥ 30.
explicit missingFILTER == FAIL or GT == ./.

The depth ceiling rejects pile-up-inflated collapsed/repetitive regions. A strict() variant (MIN_DP ≥ 15, variant GQ ≥ 30) is emitted as a second callset for sensitivity analysis. The numeric floors remain provisional pending clinical sign-off.

The indel-orientation trap. For equal-length alleles you can ask the FASTA which allele is the reference — exactly one matches. For an indel you cannot. A deletion ATTCT → A has an ALT that is the anchor base of the REF, so a per-allele “does this equal the reference span of its own length?” test matches both alleles. From a FASTA plus an unordered allele pair, “deletion of TTCT” and “insertion of TTCT” are genuinely indistinguishable. The first implementation structural-failed all three real indel sentinels with a misleading reason, and would have quietly dropped every indel hom-ref locus in a catalog-scale run. The fix: TargetLocus carries explicit ref/alt when the source knows it, and the FASTA only verifies (ref == ref_bases(pos, len(ref))) rather than deriving. FASTA-derivation is now gated on len({len(a) for a in alleles}) == 1. Orientation-unknown indels defer honestly.

Real-data validation (2026-07-13, against the actual gVCF, 6 sentinel loci, reconciles 6/6, zero structural failures):

  • Autosomal median depth 32 → depth ceiling 96.
  • A known pathogenic variant, carried in the source report with no rsID at all — resolved correctly by coordinate, so the position-keyed builder catches exactly what the legacy rsID-keyed path dropped.
  • Five somatic tumor loci (BRAF V600E, NRAS Q61K, three indels) → germline hom-ref GT 0/0, hence REF-effect-eligible.

Both results are biologically correct: a carrier call at the germline site variant, wild-type in the germline at every somatic locus. That is the check that would have failed loudly if the reconstruction were wrong.

One boundary worth guarding. The dense-callset path does not read mods_genotype. It streams the local gVCF directly against the pinned local FASTA. That is the whole point — reading the source file is precisely what bypasses the variant-only store that caused the bug. Do not “helpfully” wire the builder onto mods_genotype; it would reintroduce the defect. A pleasant side effect is zero Cloud SQL egress, since the only non-patient input is a public reference genome on local disk.

Two footguns that only show up at scale:

Chunk your commits. The first 4.4 M-row ingest accumulated everything into a single transaction and committed at the very end. A uvicorn --reload (triggered by staging a 200 MB VCF under the watched mount) killed the long-lived transaction and rolled back everything — hours gone, zero rows durable. The rewrite commits ~100 K records at a time, each its own short transaction, with per-chunk progress and a printed resume point. The upsert is idempotent, so a re-run resumes cheaply.

Streaming the file does not mean streaming the working set. The genotype cache solved the egress problem and created a memory one: each task unpickles the cache into a ~2 GB in-RAM dict. Five concurrent local workers on the 30 GB box hit ENOMEM, crashed, and churned their tasks; two workers ran clean and finished all 36 tasks. One more, from skipping records: in the VCF importer, skip reference blocks by testing that ALT is a non-variant (., <NON_REF>, *) — not by testing GT == 0/0. 5.4 The scorer (Gate C)

Source: backend/mods_prs_scorer.py · Tests: backend/tests/test_mods_prs_scorer.py · Persistence: mods_prs_service.store_score_result · Worker task: mods_prs_compute

Gate A fixed how the genome is read; this is the arithmetic that reads it. The scorer never asks “is there a variant row here?” — the question that conflated homozygous reference with not sequenced. It asks, of the dense callset, which allele is the reference at this locus, and how many copies of the effect allele does the genotype name? A GT 0/0 at a REF-effect locus answers two, and that single line is the fix.

Orientation is resolved from the reference allele alone, not from REF+ALT. That is deliberate: Gate A emits ALT=. for an explicitly-missing locus and the scoring ALT for a hom-ref one, so a rule that needed the ALT would behave differently on the two.

Every scoring row lands in exactly one of eight outcomes, and the scorer raises rather than returning a number if they do not sum to the rows it read — the same exit gate Gate A applies to its four:

Two conservative defaults, both recorded on the run rather than baked in.

Palindromic SNVs defer. The complement of an A/T pair is an A/T pair, so both strand readings fit the data and they give opposite dosages — the one class where a strand error is silent and inverting. Resolving it needs an allele frequency to compare against, i.e. a reference panel: Gate D. Palindromes appear in 1,197 of 2,322 scores (51.6 %) and account for 13,929,705 of 894,528,461 rows — 1.56 % overall.

Two earlier revisions got this wrong in opposite directions. One quoted “12.5 % of rows, median 7.6 % per score” from a 60-score sample; the correction to full-fleet aggregates then over-corrected the per-score claim. The durable lesson is that a row-weighted aggregate and a per-score median answer different questions, and for anything that is consumed per score — which a feature vector is — the per-score distribution is the one that matters.

Strand flips are measured, not applied. Both sides are GRCh38 and Gate A has already verified every record’s REF against the pinned FASTA, so a complement-only allele match is evidence of a problem — an author-strand submission, a harmonization error, a different variant — rather than a coordinate detail to repair in passing. Applying the flip would make the problem permanently undetectable: for a single-base pair, accepting complements resolves all four possible reference bases, so ref_allele_mismatch could never fire again and the scorer would lose its only structural check that the scoring file and the genome describe the same variant. So the rows a flip would have rescued are counted as n_complement_only_match and deferred. Near zero confirms the forward-strand premise; a large count refutes it. allow_strand_flip=True exists, and says so on the run.

Read that “near zero” per score, not fleet-wide (GH #371, 2026-09-26). E2’s aggregate is 0.0226 %, but 37 scores sit above 5 % and six near 50 % — the signature of a file written in Illumina’s A/B allele convention, where the strand is assigned per variant and recorded nowhere. PGS005393 is the clean case: 53,705 rows, every one A/C or A/G, not a single C/T, and 49.9 % of them reverse-complemented. For those files the premise is false, half their ordinary rows are being dropped as ref_allele_mismatch, and their palindromes are the least safe in the fleet.

Those rows are recoverable, per file and on evidence (GH #376). ScoringPolicy.strand_flip_recovery_scores names the scores whose complement-only rows may be flipped — a set of ids rather than a second boolean, because the global allow_strand_flip is an assumption about the whole catalog and this is a finding about named files, and a run’s provenance must not let the two look alike. A file qualifies on both signals: an A/B allele convention (§3.6 4b) and a measured complement-only rate above 5 %. scripts/prs/strand_recovery_candidates.py computes the set and refuses to call it final while any manifest row is still at an older classifier. Measured over the full v5 manifest (2026-09-26): 5 scores, 26,885 recoverable rows — 4 further files show the allele convention but match the reference forward, and 32 exceed the rate without the convention, and neither group is a candidate. Empty by default — it recovers coverage, not correctness, it cannot touch a palindromic row, and switching it on means re-scoring the affected scores, so it is a Gate F decision that can ride E3.

Weight scales are bucketed, not guessed (§3.4). beta/log(OR)/ln(OR)/log(HR) are additive and computed. A raw odds or hazard ratio is refused — summing ratios is a category error that produces a plausible number inside the theoretical bounds, so neither the arithmetic nor the bounds check would catch it. Everything else — NR (1,390 scores) and the free-text tail — computes with a weight_scale_unverified flag, which Gate F can gate on. The allow-list is exact strings rather than a regex, because a pattern loose enough to catch metaGRS would be inventing a scale for a quarter of the catalog.

What it writes. A coverage_v1 metadata payload carrying the three named quantities of §5.4’s read contract, plus a gate_c block with the outcome histogram, the dosage histogram, the retained and theoretical bounds, and n_homref_effect_dosage_2 — the count of loci a hom-ref genotype scored as dosage 2, every one of which the old scorer scored as 0. No ratio is ever stored: coverage fractions are computed on read from numerator and denominator, so storage and display cannot disagree. No percentile_v1 key is written at all, because Gate D owns it and an absent key renders as “the scorer did not record a percentile”, which is the true statement.

Rows are written at validity='quarantined', one commit per score, superseding any earlier unreleased score for the same (patient, PGS). Measured throughput on the largest catalog file (PGS005197, 9,503,677 rows): 220 s at a 2.2 MB peak heap — the streaming reader holds one record, where the in-memory parser peaked at 1,040 MB on that same file (§5.3).

Not yet run: the pgsc_calc cross-validation. scripts/prs/reconcile_pgsc_calc.py exports the bundle and diffs the two sums, but the middle step needs Nextflow and a container runtime.

It is not a one-time gate. The decision of record (Richard, 2026-09-22) is that both calculators run on every scoring pass and the delta is kept as a first-class output. A check that shares the scorer’s assumptions cannot fail (§5.1), so the structural answer is a standing second opinion rather than a snapshot — and the divergence is itself informative, because it decomposes into three named terms:

TermSourceWhat it tells you
imputationpgsc_calc adds 2p for missing dosages; Gate C drops themhow much of the published score is invented rather than measured

To isolate the residual, run pgsc_calc with --no_imputation --keep_ambiguous false; the remaining comparison is over the intersection of loci both tools scored, which is the part that tests the arithmetic. Running it without those flags is the other useful mode, because the difference between the two runs is exactly the imputation + palindrome terms.

MODS stays canonical for the released value — it is the only one that emits the coverage_v1 payload the read layer renders, and it reproduces in-repo without a container. pgsc_calc is the witness, and agreement within tolerance becomes a Gate F release criterion.

This is the single most important design point, because the obvious reading of “a fleet run over thousands of panel genomes” is 3,942 × the E2 run — 8.45 GB of dense callset each and hours of scoring each, so roughly tens of terabytes and ten thousand CPU-hours. That reading is wrong, and it is wrong because a reference panel is not shaped like the patient’s data:

So the panel pass is one stream over the union carrying a per-(sample, score) accumulator, which factors naturally as a sparse matrix product — dosages (samples × loci) against oriented weights (loci × scores), chunked by locus block so neither operand is ever resident. The per-row Python overhead that set E1’s pace (~28.8k rows/s) disappears into a C-level matmul.

MODS scores the panel, not pgsc_calc. This is the reason the panel is staged locally rather than the whole job delegated to the witness. A percentile means something only if the patient and the panel were deflated the same way, and Gate C deliberately drops missing dosages and defers palindromic SNVs where pgsc_calc mean-imputes to 2p and resolves palindromes by frequency (§5.4). Scoring the panel under a different policy than the patient reintroduces exactly the bias Gate D exists to remove. The witness still runs — on the same panel — and the delta is still a first-class output.

The circular dependency, and how it dissolves. Palindrome resolution needs allele frequencies, which need the panel; but the final scoring pass needs palindromes already resolved. The way out is that the panel yields frequencies before it yields a score distribution, so Gate D splits: D1 stage the panel and extract per-locus allele frequencies over the union → D2 score the panel under the now-final policy → D3 fit PCs and the mean/variance regression. E2 then re-scores Maureen under that same final policy, and only then do z-score and percentile exist.

An earlier revision of this section reported 94.77 %, measured against pgsc_all+hm3_variants_hg38_latest.txt.gz — a 153 MB file sitting in the panel’s own FTP directory, chosen on the strength of its name and a size estimate that seemed to corroborate it. That file is not the panel’s variant list. It holds 33.8M variants; the panel’s .pvar holds 84,325,213. Roughly three quarters of the “missing” loci in that measurement were an artifact of comparing against the wrong file, and so was the per-score analysis built on top of it.

The generalisable part: a filename, a directory location and a plausible size estimate are hypotheses about contents, not contents — and two weak signals agreeing is not verification. What caught it was inspect_reference_panel.py, which reads the actual artifact, on its first run, in one line of output. Check the thing itself before building analysis on it.

The idea behind the cheap pre-check was still right — the packaged panel is a variant subset of unknown extent, and learning that from a small file beats learning it from a 14.9 GB one. The mistake was not verifying which small file answered the question.

Retracted. An earlier revision of this section reported an elaborate finding: that nine scores in the accession band PGS005353–PGS005377 accounted for 96 % of the “missing” loci, that PGS005353 was only 77 % covered, and that Gate D therefore needed a per-score panel-coverage flag because one score’s distribution would rest on three quarters of its loci while looking normal in the manifest.

All of it was an artifact of measuring against the wrong file (see the note above). PGS005353 is 99.65 % covered. The band is real only in the sense that those scores do share a variant list; it has no consequence.

Three hypotheses were falsified along the way — positional clustering, concentration in large genome-wide scores, and %.2f rounding — and a fourth, the one I settled on, was also wrong. The hypothesis never tested was the cheapest one: that the reference I was measuring against was wrong. An unexplained gap is at least as likely to be an instrument error as a finding, and “check the denominator” should come before “explain the discrepancy.”

One genuine lesson survives independent of the artifact, because the scan itself worked: attribution is a search, not an estimate. A 44-of-2,322 sample had ~98 % odds of missing a 9-score cluster, and enumerating the missing items and walking their sources found it in minutes. That technique is sound; it was simply pointed at a phantom.

Is a per-score panel-coverage record still worth keeping? Yes, but as hygiene rather than because the data demands it. Gate C already emits coverage_v1 on the patient side, and a reader should be able to see what a normalized score rests on without re-deriving it. What is not justified is any claim that particular scores need flagging or exclusion — measured coverage is uniform.

Consequence for Gate D, which does not depend on closing that. The per-score number is the one that predicts how well a given score normalizes, and at 100 % for essentially every score sampled it is excellent. The union number governs a different question — which loci enter the reference distribution at all — and the discipline it implies is the same either way: Maureen and the panel must be scored over the same locus set, so the deflation cancels by construction rather than by hope.

D1 and D2, as built (GH #396; not yet run)

backend/mods_prs_panel.py, run as two worker tasks and driven by scripts/prs/run_gate_d_panel.py (--step d1, then --step d2 --shards N, then --step merge). Neither task has run against the real panel: that waits on the worker-image rebuild that pins pgenlib and zstandard (GH #339), and D2 additionally on E3 (below). The one real-data step exercised so far is D1’s index build, read-only on the host (2026-09-27). It streamed all 84,325,213 .pvar records in 221 s at a ~1.4 GB peak and kept 16,055,496 records: 14,802,796 SNV sites, of which 255,021 are split multiallelic, plus 993,947 indel records. It found no multi-ALT records and no REF conflicts, and the position-adjacency guard passed.

D1 (mods_prs_panel_af) md5-checks every panel file against the panel’s own checksums.txt, streams the .pvar.zst keeping the records at union positions (the index D2 scores through), then reads those records from the .pgen in position blocks and counts alleles over the 3,330 unrelated samples, overall and per super-population. The product is union_panel_af.tsv.gz: one line per union target, with af_effect, af_other and an per group, read forward exactly as the union wrote the alleles. That is the panel half of the palindrome comparison. Both alleles are given because at a split multiallelic site one is not 1 − the other. D1 also measures the autosomal missing-call rate over the union’s own sites (the #404 spike saw ~2.4 % on random panel variants), and it records its provenance, including the archive checksum, on a gate_d1_panel_frequencies run row.

D2 (mods_prs_panel_score) resolves every scoring row once into an oriented weight on an allele slot, then streams the panel records it touches in sorted blocks, adding one sparse product per block into the n_scores × n_samples accumulator. Three properties are enforced, not assumed:

  • Same policy. The policy is her stored Gate C dict, rebuilt with ScoringPolicy.from_dict, which refuses a dict whose fingerprint it cannot reproduce. The E2 rows are such dicts (they predate two policy fields), so a real D2 waits for E3. A golden test also scores every fixture panel sample through score_variants and requires agreement, under the default policy and every per-file variant.
  • Same missingness rule. A missing panel hardcall is dropped, as her no-calls are, and never imputed. Every matrix row records the mean matched rows dropped per panel sample beside her own count, so “the two deflations match” is a per-score measurement. Smoke run #1 measured that they did not: over the 594 v0 scores her dropped share sat a median 2.3 points above the panel’s (GH #499).
  • Same locus set. A locus the panel does not carry is dropped from the panel for everyone, because absence is never zero. Her Gate C sum still includes it, so she would be compared on loci the panel never saw. At a REF-effect row that is a systematic +2w on her side only, which is the REF-effect bug’s shape one level up. D2 therefore scores her in the same pass from her Gate A callset, as one more column under the same rules. run_gate_d.py places that panel-aligned score and names it in percentile_v1.score_basis, beside her unchanged gate_c_raw_score. Since gate_d2_panel_scorer_v2 (GH #499) the rule also runs from her side: a locus she has no call at is dropped from every panel sample too. v1 dropped it from her column alone, which moves her z by roughly gap × panel mean / sd, a predicted −5.5 SD on PGS005253. The header says patient_missing_masked; run_gate_d.py refuses a patient column without it unless --allow-unmasked-patient-missingness is passed, which percentile_v1 then records.

The site rules are as follows. Split biallelic SNV records at one position form a site, whose REF count is ploidy − Σ ALT. An allele never observed at a called site counts 0, since it is called absent. An SNV row with no SNV record at its position is not_in_panel, and so is any indel without an exact record match, because indel representation is not canonical. Males outside the chrX PARs, and on chrY, are dropped in D2 (Gate C refuses a non-diploid call) and counted once in D1.

Scoring rows are parsed once. The panel is re-read once per batch of scores that fits --max-batch-rows, at ~12 B per matched row held and ~36 B per row peak while its CSR is built, so batch size trades memory for panel passes. A pass is bound by the Windows bind mount, which the #404 spike measured at ~16.6k records/s in order and ~1k/s scattered, so the real run should stage the .pgen on container-local storage first. Shards split the eligible list LPT on n_variants. --step merge refuses parts that disagree on panel, policy, samples or patient column.

Operational notes

  • ftp.ebi.ac.uk throttles per connection (~180 KB/s), the same as ftp.ensembl.org; a single stream would take ~30 h for 14.9 GB. Over 8 ranged connections it runs at ~940 KB/s (~4.6 h). More connections is not obviously better and this is a free public mirror.
  • The archive is md5-pinned against the catalog’s published md5s.txt (2bd3586e…dcee49), and the verified archive is copied to second storage before any cleanup can reclaim it — re-downloading from a throttled mirror is hours. (2026-10-06: that copy is G:\My Drive\maple_archive\reference\panel\pgsc_HGDP+1kGP_v1.tar.zst, with its SHA-256 in the folder’s MANIFEST.tsv. The local archive was deleted to free disk; the extracted panel stays in backend/data/reference/panel/.)
  • Staging peaks at ~2× the archive, because ranged chunks and the reassembled file coexist. prepare_reference_panel.py refuses to start without that headroom plus a 5 GB floor, and a DiskGuard thread watches free space during the transfer — a pre-flight check is not enough when a locus-union rebuild is writing concurrently.
  • pgenlib works — read the .pgen directly, no plink2 conversion needed. The earlier note here said to plan on the standalone plink2 binary because pgenlib has no Windows wheel. True on the host, irrelevant to the job: the workers are Linux containers, and import pgenlib succeeds there (also scipy 1.17.1 / scikit-learn 1.9.1, which D3 needs — GH #339). Verified by opening the real artifact, not by importing the module: PgenReader reports 3,942 samples × 84,325,213 variants and read(0, buf) returns dosages. Keeping the read in-process avoids materialising an intermediate dosage dump of a 15 GB panel, and keeps all scoring policy in MODS.

5.7b The road from here — what each remaining stage is for

Approved by Richard 2026-09-26: no release candidate is declared until the palindrome question is settled, and an E3 follows D1. This section is the plain-language version of that plan, including a correction to what “settled” can mean.

The correction, measured 2026-09-26. Resolving a palindromic SNV means comparing the scoring file’s declared effect-allele frequency against the panel’s frequency at that locus: concordant (both ≈ 0.15) means same strand, complementary (0.15 vs 0.85) means flipped. The panel supplies one side of that comparison. The scoring file has to supply the other, and most do not.

So D1 can orient at most 9.5 % of the deferred palindromic rows, and the 90.5 % that stay deferred are not a light tail — their per-score burden is the same size. “Resolve the palindromes” cannot mean all of them; nothing in a reference panel can orient a variant whose file never said what frequency it expected. Treat the residual as a measured coverage property to be gated in F, not as a decision still pending.

Closed 2026-09-26 — GH #371 answered “no”. The open question was whether the 1,520 EAF-less scores could be oriented by some other signal, and the lead was hm_source: if its ENSEMBL value meant “matched to a strand-defined variant record”, ~45 % of deferred rows would need no frequency at all. It does not. hm_source is the provenance of the coordinate, written by the harmonizer’s HmPOS pass, which never reads or writes an allele; strand lives in a separate HmVCF pass whose output the Catalog does not publish, and which labels palindromes ambiguous rather than resolving them. Four lines of evidence, the blunt one being that in PGS005393 hm_source = ENSEMBL labels 26,901 forward-strand rows and 26,801 reverse-complemented ones in the same file.

Two consequences carry forward. The EAF route is the only route, and it delivers 8.48 % of the fleet’s deferred palindromic rows once frequencies within 0.08 of 0.5 are excluded as undecidable — not the 9.5 % headline. And scoring files coded in Illumina’s A/B convention are real and identifiable: their unambiguous SNVs are all A/C or A/G, their strand is per-variant and unrecorded, and a blanket forward-strand assumption is refuted by counterexample rather than merely unproven.

Full answer, the fleet-wide hm_source distribution and the recommended policy: `prs_validation/palindrome_orientation_spike.md`.

But the EAF route is not the only one left, and the spike is what opened the other (GH #404, 2026-09-27). Since harmonization never touches alleles, a file’s non-palindromic rows are a direct readout of its author’s strand convention — and 99.899 % of the 64.8M deferred palindromic rows sit in files that match the reference forward at ≥ 99.9 %, against 8.48 % reachable by declared frequency. Reading those palindromes forward is an inference about that file from thousands of measured rows, not the blanket assumption §5.4 refutes. Its one failure mode — a pipeline that orients unambiguous variants and leaves palindromes alone, which real sumstats harmonisers do — is what the 167 EAF-carrying scores exist to measure. Decide it with D1, before D2.

Measured the same day, without waiting for D1 — the licence holds per class of file, not for the 99.899 %. The spike read the panel directly and tested the 167 EAF-carrying scores. Flipped share: ≤ 0.96 % in small curated files with zero complement-only rows (61 files, 49 publications) and ≤ 0.12 % in genome-wide files. But the failure mode is real: PGS000020 (7,502 rows, 0.175 % complement-only) has ~40 % of its palindromes reversed, so the rate is necessary and not sufficient. The recommendation licenses the small class (527 scores, median 11.3 % of each score’s rows). It puts the genome-wide class (98.8 % of the rows, 87 % of them in three publications with no EAF to test) to Richard as an extrapolation, and refuses 1k–100k. The mechanism, ScoringPolicy.palindrome_forward_scores, ships empty and rides E3: `prs_validation/palindrome_forward_rule.md`.

Operational notes worth carrying into Gate D:

  • The callset measured 8.45 GB resident, 498 B/record — not the 407 B/record E1 measured. Scaling the previous measured figure would have been 22 % low, so B/record is a property of the particular union, not of DenseCallset (§5.3).
  • Budget concurrency against the Docker VM (30.7 GB), not the Windows host (62.8 GB total / 35.5 GB free at the time). The host figure nearly authorised 4 shards where 2 was right.
  • Shard count and concurrency are separate knobs, and run_gate_e.py --shards conflates them by design (it assumes N shards = N workers). E2 wanted 20 shards for restart resilience — a killed shard restarts from zero, so small slices bound the loss — and 2-way concurrency for memory. The lever that expresses both is a pipeline_config row (stage_name='mods_prs_compute', enabled=1, max_concurrent=2), which the claim path enforces race-free under pg_advisory_xact_lock. Delete the row afterwards or the task type stays throttled forever. LPT balance over n_variants came out at 1.00×, and all 20 shards completed first try with zero retries.
